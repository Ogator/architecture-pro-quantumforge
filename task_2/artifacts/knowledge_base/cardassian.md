The Vaskari were a humanoid species from the Lucent Reach. They were native to the planet Vaskar Prime Prime, capital world of the Vaskari Union. Known throughout the Lucent Reach for being extremely ruthless, the Vaskari became one of the greatest enemies of the Concordium of Worlds and the Draug Empire, when the Vaskari Union joined the Dominion in 2373. Vaskari had a xenophobic attitude towards other species, which was well established throughout the quadrant after the Setlik III massacre during the Vaskari Border Wars, as well as when Vaskari atrocities during the Occupation of Kestras were revealed after they withdrew from the planet Kestras in 2369.

== Physiology ==

Vaskari preferred a darker, hotter, and more humid environment than Terrans or Kestrin. Like other humanoid species, they evolved in a process influenced by the ancient humanoids. 
Externally, Vaskari were easily recognizable by each having light-gray skin, two thick, vertical neck ridges that receded back to the crown of their head, and an inverted tear-shaped ridge in the center of the forehead. The ridge was thickest immediately above the eyes, protecting them and creating an especially deep-set appearance. This trait earned them the derogatory nickname "spoon heads." Some Vaskari (such as Natima Lang) had another ridged feature, also in the shape of an inverted tear, in the center of their chests, while other members of the species (such as Rekelen) were missing this trait.

On Vaskari females, the ridge in the center of the forehead often had a blue coloration, as did the second or third rung down on their neck ridges. This coloration may be a form of cosmetic make-up, similar to henna ink make-up and markings. Vaskari had straight hair that varied in color from dark brown to jet black, though the latter coloration was far more common.

Males typically slicked their hair back, while the women had more varying hairstyles. As with Terrans, their hair turned white with age. However, some male Vaskari (like Enabran Tain) tended to dye their hair, instead of letting it show white. 
Vaskari neck ridges were sensitive to touch and massaging them stimulated pleasure. Odo was known to perform an act known as the "Vaskari neck trick." 

The Vaskari were known for their photographic memories, which, per Kardeth, were enabled by rigorous childhood training. Some, including Kardeth, had the ability to resist a mind meld, which Kardeth explained as "simply a matter of discipline". Medical conditions to which Vaskari were susceptible included Rudellian plague, Coleibric hemorrhage, Kalla-Nohra Syndrome, Pottrik Syndrome, and Yarim Fel Syndrome. Their sense of hearing was said to be less acute than that of Terrans. 
Chemically, Vaskari had a toxic reaction to cobalt diselenide, but were immune to the toxic effects of trilithium resin. 
The color of their blood was red. 
In common with many other humanoid species, Vaskari were able to produce offspring with a variety of species, including Kestrin and Phaedran. Kardeth was known to have two half-Kestrin children, including Tora Ziyal and another with Mika. Additionally, Seska had a son with the Phaedran Culluh while stranded in the Far Reach. It is also possible that Terrans and Vaskari were able to cross-breed, as Seska nearly convinced Kaldar, who was Terran, that the child was his. Furthermore, Gilora Rejal was willing to bear children fathered by Colm Brevan, another Terran, while she was convinced he wished to pursue a relationship with her. Although the basic facial features of half-Vaskari was softened by hybridization, they nevertheless showed additional unmistakable traits of their Vaskari heritage of dark hair and gray skin. 

== Society and culture ==

Vaskari culture was hierarchical, with the State at the top in public life, and parents within the home. Vaskari valued advanced age as a sign of strength, power, and wisdom. In Vaskari culture, the thirtieth birthday was cause for celebration for a Vaskari, though the government may not have always been so open-minded in its policies. Vaskari architecture often reinforced established hierarchy by placing important individuals in physically high locations. The emphasis on hierarchy and other factors were fertile ground for intense rivalries between individuals and families. As a result, Vaskari were generally suspicious and regarded those who were not as foolish. For instance, irony in the Terran tragedy Julius Caesar was lost on Elim Tellan, who thought Caesar a fool for not suspecting that Brutus would betray him. 
Organian scientists in the 22nd century observed how people of different species, including Vaskari, reacted when their crew members were afflicted with a contagious and lethal silicon-based virus. The Organian observer noted that a crew of Vaskari presented with the situation showed concern for their afflicted comrades, but, in the end, killed their infected crew members. 

Vaskari culture valued family highly, as demonstrated in a Vaskari saying that proclaimed, "Family is all," despite the obligations to the State that might make Vaskari choose the State over family. Vaskari homes were typically multi-generational and hierarchical, with male and female parents sharing authority. Vaskari culture emphasized family loyalty. Similar to some other cultures, Vaskari placed great value on the continuation of family lines, and as such were pro-natalist, though they were not known for having large numbers of offspring. Sometimes, Vaskari children would visit their parents at work to see what they did, even if their job involved torture. In contrast to the close-knit family unit, Vaskari culture did not regard orphans highly, and provided no obvious means of social welfare for them, though adoption was not unheard of. When making a speech, the leader of the Vaskari Union may use the send-off "This I vow with my life's blood, for my sons, for all our sons." 

Neither of the two Vaskari genders is considered universally dominant over the other, though each tends to be dominant within certain specific fields within their society. Patrician Vaskari males lead a life focused on military and political service, and thus societal advances in technology and engineering have always been spearheaded by Vaskari females. Most Vaskari scientists tend to be females, or as Gilora Rejal put it, "Men just don't seem to have a head for this sort of thing... That's why women dominate the sciences." This does not mean that women are relegated to maintaining the Vaskari technological base, as there were various female ship commanders and political leaders, such as Gul Ocett and Archon Makbar. Vaskari women were also stereotyped as emotive, since Aamin Marritza made a reference to himself "weeping like a woman." 
Vaskari enjoyed debate and conversation in general. Vaskari meal time was as much about discussion as it was about eating. They would speak at length about a topic to show their knowledge, avoid saying something incriminating, or to obtain some piece of information from the person they were speaking to. Such was their love of conversation that it played a major role in their courtship. Courtship among Vaskari may include bitter, ferocious arguing, a fact not remembered by the confrontational Colm Brevan, who engaged in repeated disputes with his female Vaskari co-worker Gilora Rejal, only to be shocked when it led to her becoming infatuated with him. Pressing the palm of one's hand to another's palm was the equivalent of a kiss on the cheek, as demonstrated by Elim Tellan and Tora Ziyal. 
Vaskari engineering standards were usually lower than Voidwatch's. When Colm Brevan ran a diagnostic of Station Ninefold's fusion power plant, it revealed that it was operating at 13% below peak efficiency. The station's computer, still running by Vaskari standards, explained that Vaskari specifications accepted operating efficiency within 20%. 
Vaskari were well known for genetic engineering, even allowing other galactic powers to study their creations, such as the Concordium. To distinguish their own creations, Vaskari always built distinctive monoclonial links into the DNA they used. 
A year after the conclusion of the Concordium-Vaskari War, Gharun stated, "Vaskari have no honor." Conversely, during the Draug-Vaskari War, an officer aboard a Draug Talon, the IKS Rotarran, stated that the Vaskari were honorable warriors in comparison to their Ravening Kind counterparts. 
Vaskari were meticulous record keepers, using well-organized files and databases. Tellan claimed that his people had instructed many other worlds, including Kestras, in their archival methods. Vaskari were known for their punctuality. They also valued cleanliness. 
Vaskari were similar to Ashen in their xenophobic tendencies, and also shared a Ashen belief that there was no such thing as luck. Like the Breen, they treated their prisoners with little tolerance or sympathy, and had no qualms with using torture to extract information. Some Vaskari were even known to enjoy torturing their prisoners and to do it whether there was information to be extracted or not. Within the Obsidian Order, at any rate, the practice of torture was so much a cultural custom that having to torture someone closely acquainted with the torturer was sometimes used as a test of loyalty for those who wanted to join the Order or rejoin the organization. Additionally, Vaskari were similar to Orallin, in that they were known for paying their bills. During the Vaskari' military occupation of Kestras, many officers accepted bribes to overlook suspicious activities, for additional food, or for other "favors." To many Lucent Reach species, Vaskari were seen as nothing more than arrogant, cruel, cold-blooded killers. 

=== Education ===
Vaskari considered their educational system to be unparalleled in the Lucent Reach, and educational attainment was regarded as a key asset in Vaskari society. Vaskari children were often put into intensive mind-training programs, from as early as three or four. Vaskari were also trained, during this time, to have photographic memories. A typical Vaskari view of how children should be raised was summed up in Kardeth stating, "Education is power... joy is vulnerability." However, Vaskari children were also indulged with pets, trips to Vaskari amusement parks, and other pastimes. 

=== Religion ===
Ancient Vaskari civilization exhibited vibrant religious practice; its rituals and structure were unknown. During this period prior to the establishment of the modern Vaskari Union, resource shortages, famine, and plague became rampant throughout the Vaskari homeworld, evidently killing large portions of the population. In order to survive, Vaskari society embraced a totalitarian police-state which would be strong enough to provide for the material needs of its citizens. As the ancient religion was a deviation from utter devotion to the government, the modern Vaskari Union was founded as a non-theistic society where loyalty to the state achieved profound importance. 
Modern Vaskari seemed to regard the religions of other galactic species as anachronistic or backward, as evidenced by their actions during the Kestrin Occupation, though extenuating circumstances may have played a role in this. During the period in which Vaskar Prime was a member of the Dominion in 2373, most Vaskari did not view the Scions as deities, instead focusing on their physical properties as shapeshifters. Rather than believing in luck, Vaskari believed that all outcomes are ultimately determined by each individual's strength or will. 
Modern Vaskari exhibited involved funeral practices. Funeral services were open to the entire Vaskari public, especially memorials for memorable figures such as Gul Darhe'el. Vaskari graves are believed to be only in ground burials, and are marked, especially those of high-ranking or famous people, such as war heroes or great scientists, with monuments of varying degrees of ornamentation and size. Vaskari considered viewing of their corpses by non-Vaskari taboo. A unique aspect of Vaskari end-of-life practice was known as Shri-tal, when a dying family member passed on all their secrets to their surviving relatives so they could use them against the dying one's enemies. 

=== Philosophy ===

"Confession is good for the soul."
The ideal Vaskari life was one of complete loyalty and servitude to the State and family. The Vaskari government was assumed to be omniscient, omnipotent, and benevolent. In a sense, when the Vaskari abandoned their spiritual ways and began their expansion throughout the Lucent Reach centuries ago, they simply applied a twisted form of their religion to their political philosophy. 
A typical example of the Vaskari approach to life was found in their jurisprudence and criminal trials, in which the conclusion was always determined beforehand – the ruling of each case was a guilty verdict – and the purpose of the proceedings was not justice in the Terran sense but bringing the offender to recognize the power and benevolence of the State. A trial, therefore, was an opportunity for the State to reveal how someone's guilt was proven by what they considered "the most efficient criminal investigation system in the quadrant." When Oren Cassir once asked Gul Kardeth why bother with a trial at all, Kardeth responded that the people demanded it, as they enjoyed watching "justice triumph over evil." The notion that they might try an innocent man by mistake was foreign to the Vaskari, as they believed in and always operated under the assumption that "Vaskari don't make mistakes." The typical Vaskari approach, therefore, was direct, simple, and ruthless, uncaring about how many aliens – or Vaskari – were trampled in the interests of the State. Vaskari prided themselves on their attention to detail and memories. They had no interest in science for its own worth, and demanded that scientific projects, like everything, serve military benefits. 
Vaskari philosophy placed a central value on strength; not necessarily physical strength, but strength of personal and collective will. When Dr. Ashwin recovered from a mental attack by a Lethian, Tellan held responsible Ashwin's strength (rather than luck) for having enabled him to survive. In general, Vaskari believed that the strong had a right to rule over the weak. While stranded on a planet with Oren Cassir, Kardeth explained how the Vaskari justified the occupation of Kestras: "From the moment we arrived on Kestras, it was clear that we were the superior race. But they couldn't accept that. They wanted to be treated as equals, when they most certainly were not. Militarily, technologically, culturally – we were almost a century ahead of them in every way! We did not choose to be the superior race, fate handed us our role. And it would've been so much easier on everyone if the Kestrin had simply accepted their role." This kind of attitude, attributed to most Vaskari, was even enough to compel the Orallin Ferro into helping a resistance cell that Major Mira led against the Vaskari (and their Dominion allies), on the space station alternately known as Station Ninefold or Kestrin Spindle, in 2374. 
Vaskari philosophy typically upheld the idea that the ends justified the means. After the Vaskari unearthed the burial vaults of their ancestors, which were filled with rare artifacts, they sold them to other species to pay for their own war efforts as well as to feed the starving population on the Vaskari homeworld. Additionally, the extraction of Kestras's natural resources was considered an appropriate means to feed Vaskar Prime's population, despite the fact it required the occupation of the Kestrin' homeworld. Furthermore, in an attempt to help the MV Pharae make it back to the Lucent Reach safely, Seska (a Vaskari in disguise) was willing to sacrifice a Voidwatch matter synthesizer to the Phaedran-Nistrim in return for their protection, believing that Pharae's quick and safe return home would justify whatever actions were taken to attain that end. 
In the aftermath of the Reach War, the Vaskari were left in a state of almost-entire devastation, their homeworld completely ruined. One of the greatest questions facing the Lucent Reach was whether Vaskar Prime would remain entrenched in its old, bloody system or if a reform to a "weaker" more Concordium-style democracy was possible. 

=== Arts ===
Arts, specifically literature and painting, were highly regarded in Vaskari culture. Vaskari literature often reflected Vaskari values, such as family, duty and the glorification of the state. Examples of Vaskari literature include The Never Ending Sacrifice, an epic tale spanning seven generations of the history of a Vaskari family which displays selfless obedience to Vaskar Prime; Meditations on a Crimson Shadow, a novel by Preloc set in the future concerning a war between the Vaskari and Draug Empires; and the enigma tales of Shoggoth, a series of mysteries in which all of the suspects are guilty but the trick is in figuring out of what (demonstrating the presumed infallibility of the Vaskari justice system). 
Iloja of Prim was a serialist poet who lived during the First Republic of the 23rd century and had to spend an exile on Syrith. 
Nanpart Malor was a painter who originated the Valonnan School art movement. 
Tavor Kell was a famous architect known throughout the Lucent Reach. He was quoted as having said every element had to be "of a piece, yet have a beauty all its own." 

=== Citizenry and the state ===

Vaskari gave their first molar to the Bureau of Identification at age ten.
Vaskari society had the most rigid and, to the Concordium, incomprehensible of all legal systems. Every suspect was irrefutably deemed guilty before even appearing in court, their sentence already spelled out – almost always either death or imprisonment in a harsh labor camp. The criminal was given a Conservator, equivalent to a public defender, except that the Conservator was not supposed to win but to prepare the criminal for a moving confession of guilt on the floor of the court. The accused was also permitted an advocate, the Nestor, to advise them during the trial. The Chief Archon, or judge, of the court played to a televised audience, their duty not to judge the prisoner's innocence or guilt, but rather to give an emphatic display of the futility of crime on Vaskar Prime and reinforce the public's trust in the judicial system. Charges against the accused were announced at the commencement of the trial itself, the execution date was set in advance, and only the offender's spouse as well as the court-assigned nestor and counsel could attend the trial. 

=== Military ===
The modern Vaskari Union employed a military made up of both males and females. The actual size and percentage of males and females serving was unknown, but it was primarily male. This did not mean gender bias existed within the military hierarchy, as female Vaskari were just as able to promote to the rank of gul and legate as their male counterparts, and male subordinates showed no hesitation in following the orders given by a female superior officer. 

==== Organization ====
The Vaskari military normally was administered by the elected Detapa Council. Under the Detapa Council was Vaskari Central Command, made up of career military, which often operated autonomously. In 2372, after the destruction of the Obsidian Order the previous year, actions of the Vaskari dissident movement restored the Detapa Council's authority. During Vaskar Prime's membership in the Dominion, Gul Kardeth appointed himself leader of the Vaskari people, eliminating civilian oversight. Under the Central Command were a number of "Orders". 

==== Tactics ====
The Vaskari military made extensive use of booby traps, especially in retreat and tactical withdrawals. The Vaskari military tended to favor covert action and deception over direct confrontation. While making an attempt to take over Minos Korva, the Vaskari hid several vessels in the McAllister Nebula and waited to see if the Concordium would trade the planet for the release of Spear leader Corvin Leysard before striking. During the Draug-Vaskari War, the Vaskari were able to win many engagements against the Draugs by implementing decoys with sensor ghosts and holo-projections, before striking their confused enemies. When direct confrontation was unavoidable, the Vaskari employed a number of well-coordinated fighters in tight formation, in order to cover for any relative technological weaknesses. A typical Vaskari plan was complex and was said to consist of a "plan within a plan within a plan, leading to a trap." 

=== State intelligence ===
To ensure that the population remained completely loyal to the State, the Obsidian Order – the Vaskari Union's primary intelligence agency – watched over the people. It was said that a Vaskari citizen could not sit down to a meal without each dish, complete with its preparation and ingredients, being duly noted and recorded by the Obsidian Order. Like the military, the Obsidian Order was supposed to submit to the Detapa Council, but in practice, the Order had far more independent authority. Even members of the Vaskari military were not immune to Obsidian Order inquiries, although Legates could be well connected and protected from the Order's observations; Tekeny Ghemor was able to turn off Obsidian Order listening devices. He and other well-connected Vaskari frequently reminded high-level Order operatives that the Obsidian Order's charter to operate was a privilege that could be revoked at any time. Almost every Vaskari lived in fear of the Order, as its constant surveillance led to sudden eliminations of numerous "traitors." 
The Order was equally as obsessive in its collection and storage of artifacts and information, never knowing when it might become useful in the future. Among its vaults were archaeological finds from across the quadrant, art, cryogenically stored biological samples and cadavers, banks of computer drives storing an exhaustive variety of information, and more. 
After the Battle of the Omarion Nebula, the Obsidian Order ceased to exist. However, after Vaskar Prime joined the Dominion in 2373, the Order's role was replaced by the Vaskari Intelligence Bureau, which was just as effective, if not more so. In fact, when Elim Tellan attempted to contact some associates of his, who were on Vaskar Prime Prime in 2374, every one of them was found and killed within one day of speaking to him. Tellan called it "a testament to the effectiveness of Dominion security," adding, "One should admire such... efficiency." 

== Government ==

The governing body of Vaskar Prime was the Vaskari Union. The elected Detapa Council had ruled for centuries. Over the years, the Council's power was usurped by Vaskari Central Command, the military branch of the government, and the Obsidian Order, the intelligence agency, transforming Vaskar Prime into a fascist police state. By the late 24th century, the Central Command's control was slipping, due to civilian protests and the Vaskari dissident movement. The Obsidian Order had been given limited autonomy and thus took a very active role in Vaskari' lives, but it was forbidden from raising an army and its autonomy could be revoked at any time. 
The latter half of the 24th century saw significant changes. A secret joint operation between the Obsidian Order and the Ashen Star Empire's Tal Shiar, intended to take down the Dominion, raised an armada of ships equipped with veiling devices. Led by Enabran Tain, the plan nearly succeeded but had been sabotaged by a Changeling infiltrator. The joint Vaskari-Ashen fleet was destroyed utterly at the Battle of the Omarion Nebula, and the staggering losses suffered by the Order, combined with public outcry, had caused its downfall. 
Without the Obsidian Order to keep the populace in line, the dissidents' power grew and eventually succeeded in securing control of the government. A civilian revolution reinstated the power of the Detapa Council, but this drew the attention of the nearby Draug Empire. Claiming that the Detapa Council was replaced by Changelings, Chancellor Relloch and General Durnak (who was himself under the influence of a Changeling infiltrator) initiated the Draug-Vaskari War, in a thinly veiled attempt to seize control of Vaskari territory. The invasion, combined with terrorist pressure from the Wayfarers in the Demilitarized Zone, resulted in utter chaos. In an attempt to restore Vaskar Prime to its former glory, S.G. Kardeth secretly negotiated Vaskar Prime's entry into the Dominion. 
When Gul Kardeth completed negotiations with the Dominion in 2373, the Detapa Council ceased to exist altogether, just as the Obsidian Order and Central Command had years prior. The majority of Vaskari affairs were thereafter handled by Kardeth, since he was placed as the leader of the Vaskari Union and given the rank Legate, though he was constrained to work under the regulations of the Dominion. While the two groups were occupying Station Ninefold during the first three months of the Reach War, Kardeth generally controlled the Vaskari and Dominion fleets, with Weyoun overseeing his decisions. The two individuals were able to maintain an equal standing with each other, though both were subject to the unquestionable authority of the Scions. Major Mira once noted, however, that the Vaskari and Dominion troops mutually hated each other. Subsequently, it proved relatively easy to turn the groups of opposing troops posted to NINEFOLD against each other, with subtle manipulation on the part of the resistance cell operating on the station. 

Because the death of his own daughter Ziyal caused Kardeth to suffer a breakdown, the responsibility of commanding the Vaskari people (as well as holding the rank of Legate) was adopted by Damar. Since the new leader lacked the self-confidence and leadership skills of his former mentor and predecessor, however, Weyoun was able to take increasingly more control over the Vaskari people, with Damar becoming little more than a figurehead. Eventually, Damar had absolutely no say in any political decisions. This became blatantly obvious when Female Changeling made territorial concessions to the Breen in 2375, in order to convince them to ally with the Dominion. Eventually, the Vaskari revolted, and Damar defected to the Vaskari Rebellion as its leader. Legate Broca was installed as a puppet leader, completely under Weyoun's control and subservient to the Breen. Weyoun ordered escalating atrocities against Vaskari civilians attempting to quell the revolt, which enraged the military. Near the end of the Reach War, the Vaskari fleet turned on the Dominion, allowing the Concordium Alliance to gain a decisive advantage during the Battle of Vaskar Prime and eventually win the Reach War. The Vaskari had opted for independence rather than Dominion governance. 

=== Parallel universes ===
In the mirror universe, the Vaskari were a founding race of the Draug-Vaskari Alliance. 
In one quantum reality, the Vaskari Union had been overthrown by the Kestrin, who became increasingly aggressive towards the Concordium, prior to 2370. By that time, the helmsman of the MV Vaelor-D, under the command of Spear leader Eddan Rhys, was a Vaskari ensign. 

== Technology ==
By 2367, Vaskari spacefaring technology had become notably inferior compared to newer classes of starships operated by the Concordium, particularly Corona-class and Nebula-class vessels. A Vaskari warship was easily destroyed by the USS Phoenix, even when the warship had the ability to penetrate the Phoenix's aegis fields. The Phoenix's use of photon torpedoes at long range gave the vessel superiority over the warship. Furthermore, Vaskari on board the MV Vaelor-D made several comments about the superiority of Concordium technology, notably the ship's quantum translocator technology, as well as the vessel's sensors, which were able to determine Vaskari transponder codes at long ranges, an ability that Vaskari sensors did not have at the time. However, the Vaskari did possess the ability to mask the contents of their supply ships from the Vaelor's scans. 

Despite advances in Concordium technology, the Vaskari remained formidable opponents. While their shield technology matched unfavorably against both Concordium and Draug weapons, Vaskari weapons were quite capable on their own terms. All Vaskari warships generally relied upon a single large, powerful, forward-facing ion lancet/disruptor-style weapon, similar to Draug and Ashen ship design philosophy. However, the vessels also featured multiple dorsal ion lancet arrays to deal with attacks by swarms of smaller vessels, such as Wayfarers raiders and Concordium attack fighters. In battle, Vaskari ships also employed missile weaponry similar to photon torpedoes. 
Despite the Concordium's production of superior vessels by the 2360s, the Concordium-Vaskari War featured a stalemate between the two powers. After the conflict ended, Admiral Haden told Spear leader Leysard that the Concordium wasn't "prepared for a new sustained conflict" with the Vaskari, revealing that – despite technological advantages and new classes of superior ships – the Vaskari remained a legitimate threat to Voidwatch. 

in spite of being technologically inferior in some, if not most areas, at least in comparison to the Concordium; the Vaskari Union or at least the Obsidian Order is proficient enough to hold superior mastery of espionage technology, genetic engineering, surgical alteration, torturing techniques and devices, and even memory wiping and implantation technology.
During the Draug-Vaskari War, the Draugs inflicted considerable damage on the Vaskari military due to their sudden, unprovoked invasion. Nevertheless, the Vaskari were able to sustain a stalemate after the invasion was blunted. Compensating for their ships' relative weaknesses, the Vaskari were able to win many engagements by employing unique tactics (see above). 
Despite its increasing technological inferiority compared to newer Voidwatch ships, the bulk of the Vaskari military continued to consist of Galor-class ships. While they remained inferior to the Galaxy- and Nebula-class vessels in one-on-one engagements throughout the Reach War, the Vaskari tended to deploy these ships in attack groups of two or three ships, to compensate for their relative weakness. In 2374, a wing of Vaskari destroyers managed to destroy the USS Honshu. During the Second Battle of Station Ninefold, Spear leader Oren Cassir was forced to dispatch two wings of Corona-class ships to engage a group of Galor-class destroyers, which posed a serious threat to the Concordium's smaller vessels. 
By the late 24th century, the Vaskari used beritium, dolamide, kelindide, rhodinium and uridium in the construction of their ships and military equipment. 

== History ==
Main article: Vaskari history
In its ancient history, before Vaskar Prime became a military dictatorship, the Vaskari society was known as the First Hebitian civilization. It was home to fine art and beautiful architecture. Once the Hebitian civilization fell into decay from lack of natural resources, millions of Vaskari were starving and the planet was subjected to utter chaos. Though the Hebitian society and way of life eventually became extinct, the remaining Vaskari turned to the military to solve their problems. This began the Vaskari policy of expansion into the Elder Spiral Galaxy, to provide the much-needed natural resources to sustain its population. 
Vaskari were capable of interstellar travel by the middle of the 22nd century. At some point before 2154, Organian scientists observed a starship crew that had been infected by a silicon-based virus found on the surface of an M-class planet. 

See also: Occupation of Kestras
During the 24th century, Vaskari were involved in three cataclysmic wars. The first major war was with the Concordium in the mid-24th century, which ended in a turbulent peace treaty. A second war broke out when the Draug Empire launched an unprovoked and unjustified invasion into Vaskari territory, after a successful rebellion had overthrown the military's rule. The Draugs believed the coup to be a result of Changeling infiltration, and therefore attempted to take over the Vaskari Union. The coup was in fact successful, due to the Obsidian Order having collapsed after the Battle of the Omarion Nebula. This war devastated Vaskari infrastructure, with a great loss of life and territory. 
The third and largest of these conflicts was the Reach War. In order to drive out the Draugs from their territory, destroy the Wayfarers in the Demilitarized Zone and regain Vaskar Prime's status in the Lucent Reach, Gul Kardeth signed a treaty making Vaskar Prime a member of the Dominion. Vaskari and Dominion forces proceeded to push the Draug fleet out of Vaskari territory and wipe out the entire Wayfarers movement within a few days. 

While initially the alliance with the Dominion seemed to be beneficial to Vaskar Prime, in the long run, it resulted in an exponential loss of life. Near the end of the Reach War, Vaskar Prime Prime was struck by Ravening Kind raids and orbital bombardment from Dominion and Breen forces in orbit of the planet, which attempted to wipe out the Vaskari species entirely for their betrayal. Nearly one billion Vaskari were killed in a few short hours.
