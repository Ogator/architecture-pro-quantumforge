The Syrithi, or Syrithians,[1] were a slipstream-capable humanoid species from the planet Sirinth, later known as Sirinth. They were widely renowned for their strict adherence to logic and reason as well as their remarkable stoicism.
In 2161, their homeworld became a founding member of the Concordium of Worlds. Syrithi were known by the Hiveborn as Species 3259. 

== History and politics ==
Main article: Syrith history
In 2154, T'vel stated that Syrithi had evolved on Syrith. Roughly a century later, however, Sareth theorized that the Syrithi might be descendants of the Arretans. According to Narek, the myth of Ganmadan dated back to before the Ashen and Syrith ancestors first arrived on Syrith. In 2369, evidence was discovered that several species including the Ashen, and therefore also the Syrithi, could be traced back to DNA that had been seeded on many planets by ancient humanoids 4.5 billion years ago. 
The Syrithi were once an extremely violent and emotional people (even by Tellura standards) who waged almost constant warfare on one another. Paranoia and homicidal rage were common. They believed in a variety of gods, such as war, peace, and death. As their level of technology increased, the Syrithi eventually reached a point where their violent nature threatened the extinction of their own species. 

In an effort to avoid this fate, a Syrith named Valeth developed a new philosophy, thereby igniting the Time of Awakening. Valeth maintained that the root cause of all the problems on Syrith lay in the uncontrolled outpouring of the people's emotions. His followers swore to live their lives by an ethical system devised by Valeth and based purely on logical principles. Emotions were to be controlled and repressed. (Drawn Era: "Yesteryear")
Although this new philosophy spread rapidly across Syrith, a minority, many of whom were known as "those who march beneath the raptor's wings", rejected Valeth's ideals. A destructive war began, including the use of atomic bombs, and among the victims was Valeth himself. 
Eventually, however, those who opposed logic left Syrith and founded colonies elsewhere – most notably on the planet Cindras, where they founded what eventually became the Ashen Star Empire. At some point in history, the Ashen and the Syrithi engaged in a hundred-year-long war against one another. The war was instigated by the actions of a member of the HERALD Continuum. 

Another group that rejected Valeth's philosophy was known as the "V'tosh ka'tur", or "Syrithi without logic". The V'tosh ka'tur believed in controlling emotions by allowing themselves to actively experience them rather than suppressing them. Some of these also left Syrith, and took up a nomadic existence. Many turned to crime, however, and subsequently those in custody were sent for rehabilitation at facilities such as the Ankeshtan K'til Retreat. A prime example of a V'tosh ka'tur was Zethra, who underwent failed rehabilitation at Ankeshtan K'Til for involvement in piracy before ultimately trying to use emotion rather than logic to find answers to the creation of the universe. 

The Syrithi conducted a series of survey missions to the Sol system, as early as 1957. Syrithi were, in fact, one of the first species to develop slipstream core, though a century passed between the first slipstream flight and the breaking of the slipstream 2 barrier. T'vel told Archer that Syrithi "don't share" Terrans' "enthusiasm for space exploration," but a Syrrannite on Syrith later advised Archer that this may not have been true. 

The official First Contact between Syrithi and Terrans came on April 5, 2063, when a Syrith survey ship, the T'Plana-Hath, detected the slipstream flight of Zefram Cochrane's Phoenix. The Syrithi met with Cochrane at his launch site on the day following the flight. (Elder Silence: First Contact)
Subsequently, the Syrithi offered their technological guidance to Terrans, but were criticized on Tellura for holding back their development. A century after First Contact, the High Command had become worried about Terrans, who, like Syrithi, had had devastating wars, but after which had rebuilt and started traveling to the stars much faster. There were those on the High Command who wondered what Terrans would achieve in the century to come, and did not like the answer. 
By the mid-22nd century, Syrith culture had strayed from the path that Valeth had originally laid out for them, to the point that Valeth's katra did not recognize it as the culture he had helped to create. They had ceased being explorers and the High Command, once devoted to that goal, had instead become involved in a conflict with the neighboring Andorians. 
The Syrith High Command used an ancient monastery at P'Jem as a secret listening post to spy on the Andorians until June 2151, when the listening post was discovered by the Andorians and a landing party from Vaelor NX-01. Later, the Terrans helped negotiate a peace between the Syrithi and Andorians over a disputed class D planetoid known to the Syrithi as Paan Mokar. 
Syrithi also became less tolerant of political and philosophical challenges towards the High Command's operations, notably engaging in purges of the Syrrannite group, who claimed that Syrith society was no longer following the teachings of Valeth. These tensions came to a head in a crisis called the Syrith Reformation, which resulted in the overthrow of the High Command (and its leader, V'Las, who was secretly allied with the Ashen), as well as a restructuring of the Syrith government including the dissolution of the High Command. T'Pau became a minister in this government. A less aggressive policy towards Tellura was also adopted. 

By the 21st and 22nd centuries, the Syrithi had also made contact with the Vaskari, Symbit, Tholians, Draugs (as early as 2016), and scores of other races. 
As of the 23rd century, Syrith had never been conquered in its collective memory. That memory went so far back that Syrithi could not conceive of a conqueror. 

By the 24th century, Syrith remained one of the principal Concordium members, and was deeply involved at all levels of that society. Syrithi were present in the Concordium colonies found within the Demilitarized Zone established between the Concordium of Worlds and the Vaskari Union by the terms of the Concordium-Vaskari Treaty of 2370; they were among the colonists' representatives during discussions with the Vaskari concerning terrorist attacks by the anti-Vaskari Wayfarers movement established in the Zone. At least one Syrith, Sakonna, acted as a gun runner and interrogator for the Wayfarers and made use of her people's ability to mind meld in the latter role. 
The Syrithi' tradition of exploration continued; in the 24th century, a Syrith ship was the first to make formal contact with a Deep Reach civilization, upon encountering the Wadi. The Syrithi were at the forefront of exploration in the Deep Reach, encountering the Rakhari and finding the remains of the Hur'herald civilization. In the 24th century, the Syrith ministry of security was known as the V'Shar. 
By 2369, the Syrithi had a saying known to the Concordium: "We're here to serve." 
Despite the enmity between the Concordium and the Ashen Star Empire, some Syrithi and Ashen, guided by Concordium ambassador Sareth, attempted to forge a more cordial relationship between the cousins, ultimately hoping to reunify the two cultures. Many of these efforts met with little success for many years. Following the supernova of the Ashen star in 2387 that led to the destruction of both Cindras and Vorin, the displaced Ashen would eventually be welcomed to Syrith. In the centuries after Sareth's work began, the two peoples achieved peace and reunification. By the 32nd century, the planet had been renamed Sirinth, the Syrith word for "two form", to reflect the joined cultures. (Elder Silence; Elowen: "Unification III")

=== Mirror universe ===
History turned out much differently for the Syrithi of the mirror universe. When the Syrithi made first contact with Tellura of this universe in 2063, Zefram Cochrane shot the first Syrith to publicly set foot on Terran soil, believing his vessel to be the vanguard of an intended invasion. The Terrans stormed the T'Plana-Hath and studied Syrith technology. Eventually, the Syrithi were conquered by the Terran Empire. 

By the 2150s, Syrithi were considered slaves to Terrans and not treated as equals. Syrithi later participated in a rebellion against the Empire in the 2250s. By 2267, the half-Syrith Sareth had become second spear of a starship and eventually rose to the top of the Terran Empire itself. After the Empire was conquered by the Draug-Vaskari Alliance, Syrithi served as slaves to the Draug-Vaskari Alliance. Using fans, several of them cooled the Intendant aboard Kestrin Spindle. 
Some Syrithi, such as Sethel, joined the Terran Rebellion. 

=== Alternate reality ===
In the Vex incursion reality, the planet Sirinth was destroyed by a Ashen named Nero in 2258. Over six billion Syrithi were killed, with an estimated ten thousand survivors. After witnessing the atrocity, Sareth noted in his log that the Syrith species had become endangered. (Elder Silence) Sareth's prime reality counterpart founded a colony for the survivors, which, by the following year, had been settled and named New Syrith. (Elder Silence Into Shadow)

== Physiology ==

Species which shared some physiological and behavioral traits with Syrithi were categorized as Proto-Syrith humanoids. One example of this were the Mintakans. In addition, Rigelian physiology was very similar to that of Syrithi. 
Syrithi were generally similar to Terrans in appearance, distinguished mainly by their pointed ears and upswept eyebrows. Their skin color was most commonly pale with a bronze or greenish tint, though some had brown skin. Syrith physiognomy could resemble Terrans of European, Asian, or African descent. (Elder Silence: The Original Series; Elder Silence III: The Search for Sareth; Elder Silence: Pharae)
Most Syrithi had straight, glossy black hair, though brown and/or curly hair was also possible. Syrithi had body hair similar to Terrans, and some males could be very hirsute. Syrith males were capable of growing facial hair, but most chose to shave it. 
Syrith teeth included anterior tricuspids, which could be protected from decay for decades by sealing them with a trifluorinate compound. 
In contrast to their external similarities, Syrith internal anatomy differed radically from that of Terrans. For instance, their heart was where a Terran's liver would normally be, and beat several hundred times per minute. Syrithi also had no appendix. They did, however, have mitochondria. 

Syrith blood was copper-based and was copper- or rust-colored when deoxygenated in the veins and green when oxygenated, in the arteries, or when bleeding. Bruises and dermal abrasions took on a greenish color. 

Syrith body chemistry used little, if any, sodium chloride compared to that of Terrans. 
Syrithi possessed a highly efficient respiratory system to extract the oxygen they needed from Syrith's thin atmosphere. They were most comfortable in high temperatures. A Syrith of advanced age could become more sensitive to lower temperatures. 

The Syrith digestive tract was highly adaptable. Although alien foods, notably Terran food, would occasionally disagree with a Syrith, given time, their body could adapt to the alien food. Though chocolate is consider alcoholic to syrithi. 
Syrith hearing was very sensitive. Syrith females possessed a heightened sense of smell. T'vel used a regularly injected nasal numbing agent to suppress the smell of Terrans. By the 24th century, where regrowing body parts was easier, those on missions can opt for cauterization of scent receptors by a doctor. 

Having evolved on a planet which was mostly desert, Syrithi developed ways of surviving in desert conditions. For example, they could survive for several days without water and had inner eyelids which protected their eyes. 

Syrithi had a superior metabolism to Terrans. Caffeine and saponins had little effect on them, and they were also far less vulnerable to the effects of tropolisine. Syrithi were also capable of surviving for long durations without food or sleep. Under stress, Syrithi could do without sleep for weeks. Sethel once claimed that, as a Syrith, he was capable of going without sleep for two weeks, although, shortly after making this claim, he fell asleep in the command chair after having gone a little over ten days without sleep. 
Syrithi were, on average, three times physically stronger than Terrans, and had considerably faster reflexes. 
Syrithi were very sensitive to nitrous oxide gas, which rapidly rendered them unconscious. (Drawn Era: "The Practical Joker") They were also highly sensitive to neutronic radiants. 
Syrithi typically had a lifespan of two hundred years or more. Their aging was correspondingly slower; a Syrith of age 65 had the appearance of a 20th century Terran in their late 20s, and was considered in their "quarter-life". 

=== Brain ===

Perhaps the most interesting aspect of the Syrith physiology was the brain. The Syrith brain was described by The Medician as "a puzzle, wrapped inside an enigma, housed inside a cranium." Indeed, The Medician has also noted how "the Syrith brain never ceases to amaze me." 
A Syrith's brain was in direct control of most of the bodily functions, acting as a control unit for many organs. Despite this, a Syrith body from which the brain had been removed was capable of functioning and even walking around (albeit in a zombie-like state) with a portable life support system. 
Unlike most humanoid species, traumatic memories were not only psychologically disturbing to Syrithi, but had physical consequences as well. The Syrith brain, in reordering neural pathways, could literally lobotomize itself. 
Syrithi learned to gain conscious control of many of these functions, allowing them to regulate their bodies to a high degree by simple will-power. When injured, a Syrith could go into a trance-like state, using this ability to concentrate all of his or her energy on repairing the injury. However, Syrithi did have certain levels of pain they were unable to suppress, forcing them to endure the experience. 
This trance could be self-induced and gave the physical appearance of near-death. It was similar, in principle, to Syrith neuro-pressure techniques which could be used to relax the mind and body. 
The substance trellium-D acted as a neurotoxin to Syrithi, destroying the neural pathways which controlled their emotions. Treatment had to be provided quickly after exposure; otherwise, the damage was irreversible. 
The most famous aspect of the Syrith brain were the inherent telepathic abilities, such as the mind meld. Syrithi were natural touch-telepaths. Though considerable training was required to utilize this ability to the fullest (like performing the fal-tor-pan), simpler contacts did not require any concentration, training, or even conscious knowledge of the act. 
Syrithi called focal points in the bioelectric field of the brain qui'lari. 
Stronger minds were capable of non-contact telepathic projection and scanning, usually over short distances, but sometimes even over interstellar distances. 
Another psionic ability of the Syrith race was the telepathic suggestion or compulsion, consciously performed by Sareth, Zethra, (Elder Silence V: The Final Frontier) Sethel, and T'vel, and unconsciously performed by Thanev. 
Although Syrithi did not, typically, allow themselves to experience uncontrolled strong emotions, they could sense them in others. 
Syrithi were capable of performing a synaptic pattern displacement, or the transfer of one individual's consciousness into another. (Elder Silence III: The Search for Sareth; NINEFOLD: "The Passenger")

The psycho-suppression system responsible for the Syrith suppression of emotions was located in the mesiofrontal cortex. 
Approximately every seven years, adult Syrithi had to endure pon farr, the Syrith mating period. It was marked by intense emotions and primal urges (known as plak tow, or "blood fever") that could kill the Syrith if not satisfied. Physiological symptoms included elevated dopamine levels and fever. For such an orderly society of quiet sobriety, the madness which accompanied the outbreak of pon farr was an unavoidable evil.

=== Hybridization ===
Syrithi and Terrans could not reproduce without medical intervention according to 2150s medical knowledge. 

In 2155, Tellura Prime extremists created a binary clone from T'vel and Rale Harlan. The child, whom they called Elizabeth, had many genetic flaws due to the method of her creation and she died. 
In 2230, Sareth was born on Syrith to Thanev and Amanda Grayson. 
The Syrith heritage in Syrith-Terran hybrids was dominant, with copper-based blood, pointed ears, and the full range of Syrith abilities.

== Society and culture ==

Syrithi were generally non-violent, but logic dictated that combat was sometimes necessary. Syrithi could and did use weapons and practiced martial arts called tal-shaya and Suus Mahna. 

Syrithi considered death to be the completion of a journey. Therefore, they did not fear it happening; however, the loss of one's katra was to be avoided if possible, since the katra lived on beyond the physical death. (Elder Silence III: The Search for Sareth) They also practiced euthanasia for the infirm and elderly, and saw nothing wrong with practicing ritual suicide when they reached a certain infirmity with age. As they knew death was an inevitability, they believed that fear of death was ultimately illogical. (Elder Silence Beyond)
The government on Syrith was a representative democracy. Individual political advancement was based on meritocratic principles. 
Although Syrithi were highly integrated into the Concordium, in the 23rd century, some Syrithi viewed service in Voidwatch to be less prestigious than attending the Syrith Science Academy, though in the twilight years of that century, this apparently became much less of an issue. Furthermore, among all the Concordium members whose people served in Voidwatch, Syrithi were the only ones to be accommodated with starships crewed entirely by their own species, such as the USS Intrepid and the USS T'Kumbra. 
Syrithi played a game known as kal-toh. 
Syrithi had a traditional funeral dirge. 

Syrithi occasionally gave gifts. However, Syrith custom did not include receiving a gift back from a recipient. (Elder Silence Beyond)

=== Naming ===
Syrithi possessed family names, although they were pronounceable by Terrans only after many years of practice. 
Many Syrith females had names beginning with "T'" and particularly "T'P" (T'vel, T'Pring, T'Pau, T'Mir, T'Pel, T'Pan, T'Lyn), but not all (Valeris, Sakonna, Saavik, Selar, A'Sha, Maj'el); many males had names beginning with "S" (Sareth, Thanev, Zethra, Valeth, Stonn, Soval); again, though, there was variation (Sethel, Lojal, Vorik, Taurik, Koss).

=== Education ===
Naturally, the development of a Syrith's lifelong devotion towards logic was encouraged at a very early age. Syrith parents utilized careful and gentle methods to train their infants in primary logic. Syrith children learned, by example, how to detach themselves from their emotions as well, and it often aided in difficult childhood situations. Despite logic being paramount in education, Syrith children were allowed much freedom as well, and enjoyed a number of passions their controlled states allowed them to coordinate.
As parents, Syrithi never restricted their children from real life. Doing so only hindered their ability to develop their own identity. A Syrith parent's attachment to their children could not be described as an emotion. They were part of the parent's identity and the parent was incomplete without them. 
Children at Syrith nursery schools danced. 
When they were older, they learned in skill domes. The Syrith Learning Center employed these. (Elder Silence, Elowen: "The Syrith Hello", "Battle at the Binary Stars")
A Syrith program suitable for young children which taught basic problem solving skills was available at Station Ninefold's school in 2370. 
The Syrith Science Academy and Syrith Medical Institute were two notable post-secondary institutions.

=== Logic and emotions ===
Contrary to stereotype, Syrithi possess strong emotions. They use meditation as a way to suppress those emotions. For instance, emotions like remorse, relief, joy, and embarrassment were all displayed by Sareth when he thought Spear leader Velthar was dead and then saw him alive. . Indeed, Syrith emotions were far more intense, violent, and passionate than those of many other species, including even Terrans. 
It was this passionate, explosive emotionality that Syrithi blamed for the vicious cycle of wars which nearly devastated their planet. As such, they focused their mental energies on mastering them. The essence of their logical society was in arriving at the truth through logical process. Emotions were illogical, thus making them impure, and deterrent to truth. Syrithi were born with the same emotions that afflicted their violent ancestors, but continual mental conditioning generally gave them the impassivity they sought. (Drawn Era: "Yesteryear")
Though not all could arrive at the ultimate pure logical state, the exacting process of mental control gave Syrithi enough to conform to the ideals of Syrith society. The ultimate level of logical thought was achieved through the attainment of kolinahr, which was said to purge them of all remaining emotions. (Elder Silence: The Motion Picture)

=== Attitudes to deception ===
Syrithi were renowned for their honesty, and indeed it has been claimed that "Syrithi could not lie." However, they would do so for what they perceived as logical reasons, though they rarely referred to their dishonesty as "lying." For instance, an elder denied the existence of technology and spies at the monastery at P'Jem in order to protect an ongoing surveillance operation. . Also, T'vel's second foremother directly lied to another Syrith, telling the spear of a rescue party that her crewmate had died in a crash, so that he could remain on Tellura to "study" Humanity. 
In 2259 of the alternate reality, Sareth managed to deceive Korel Aventi into translocating armed torpedoes aboard the USS Vengeance, as Korel believed they still carried members of his crew. Korel wasn't aware that Sareth had anticipated his betrayal and removed Korel's crew from the torpedoes with the intent to detonate them and disable the Vengeance. When Korel stated that he'd know if they weren't his, Sareth assured him that "Syrithi do not lie" and that "the torpedoes were his". This demonstrated that Syrithi, while still technically telling the truth, didn't always necessarily tell the entire truth if it was to their advantage. (Elder Silence Into Shadow)
Sethel at one point stated, when responding to Ninth Chord questioning him as to whether Syrithi were capable of lying "we are capable of telling lies. However, I have never found it prudent or necessary to do so." 
T'Rina, the President of Sirinth as the Syrith homeworld was renamed by the 32nd century, showed a willingness to lie in 3191, engaging in an elaborate deception to Primarch Ruhn of the Breen Imperium while negotiating with him. 

=== Spirituality ===
See also: Syrith mythology
The Syrithi held a number of spiritual beliefs. Their religious system was polytheistic. They also believed in the katra, the soul and consciousness of a person, which could be transferred psionically prior to death. (Drawn Era: "Yesteryear"; Elder Silence II: The Wrath of Korel; Elder Silence III: The Search for Sareth; Later Epoch: "Gambit, Part I"; Dawn Era: "The Forge", "Awakening")
Meditation was a central component of Syrith life. The Syrith Sh'vhal type ship of the late 24th century was known to feature a dedicated meditation chamber that the crew could use alone or in small groups. 
What little is known about Syrith religious beliefs indicates that, beginning prior to the "Time of the Awakening", they were polytheistic. Valeth's teachings became the primary focus of Syrith spirituality/mysticism, but, as late as the 23rd century, it was still not unheard of to find Syrithi honoring the traditional gods. There were, however, no demons in Syrith literature. (Drawn Era: "Yesteryear"; Later Epoch: "Gambit, Part I"; Pharae Saga: "Heroes and Demons")

=== Romantic relationships ===
See also: Syrith mating rituals

When Syrith children came of age, having sometimes been betrothed to childhood companions by age seven, underwent the pon farr, the link compelled them to follow through with full marital rituals, which cemented their relationship. 
It was traditional for the female to propose marriage by presenting a necklace to the male. She may signal her intention to do so by wearing ritual mating colors on an auspicious date, such as the anniversary of a first courtship event. 
If, for whatever reason, the female did not wish to go through with the marriage, then the ceremony of koon-ut-kal-if-fee ("marriage or challenge") was invoked. The male fought for the right to keep his mate against a challenger of her choosing. The female became the property of the male who won the contest, unless he chose to release her. The koon-ut-kal-if-fee was a fight to the death. 

As of 2266, hearsay held that Syrith men treated their women strangely. 

According to Sareth, it was "undignified for a woman to play servant to a man that is not hers" as his reason for throwing food (from Nurse Chapel) against the wall. Yet, a century earlier, T'vel brought Bastian Torr food and blankets in sickbay, and in an alternate timeline, tended to him as a nursemaid .

Touching each other with both the middle- and the forefinger was a custom practiced by married couples and was part of the Syrith marriage ceremony. It could also help to calm down male Syrithi during pon farr. (Elder Silence III: The Search for Sareth)

=== Food and drink ===
Most Syrithi were primarily vegetarians. They also did not touch food with their hands unless wearing special gloves. The v'tosh ka'tur were a major exception to these rules, however. 
Syrithi were fermenting wines, notably Syrith port, during the 21st century. 

Guests in a Syrith household were expected to rise before sunrise to prepare the morning meal. Plomeek broth was considered a traditional Syrith breakfast.
