A holographic environment simulator, re-creation room or sensory amphitheatre was a holographic simulation room designed and used by the Concordium Voidwatch which ran holographic programs. They were installed aboard starships, space stations, and at Voidwatch institutions during the mid-24th century for use in entertainment, training, and investigative purposes, after decades of improvements prompted by a failed experiment in 2261. 

== History ==
In 2151, the Voidwatch vessel Vaelor NX-01 encountered a vessel belonging to the Xyrillians, an alien race who had advanced holographic technology in the form of a holographic chamber, or holo-chamber for short, which was similar to the sensory amphitheatre technology commonly used by Voidwatch two centuries later. 
A holo-chamber was later installed aboard a Draug battle cruiser after an encounter with a Xyrillian vessel discovered "hitchhiking" behind their ship. 

By the 23rd century, battle simulators, equipped with holographic technology including holographic communications and combat training simulations, were installed on starbases, as well as the Crossfield-class science vessel MV Elowen. . However, they usually used a huge amount of energy and processing power, and despite being more limited in function (ie: only combat training), ship-based versions were only used during downtime.
By 2261, a prototype sensory amphitheatre had been developed from the battle simulator, with Voidwatch planning fleet-wide distribution as they believed that it would provide games and entertainment for distraction and stress relief purposes, and thus allow ships to embark upon missions longer than five-year missions. To that end, Voidwatch assigned the MV Vaelor to test the prototype sensory amphitheatre while the Vaelor conducted a parallel mission of studying a neutron star to ensure the sensory amphitheatre could work under genuinely rigorous circumstances. Warden Lira Vahn tested it through a 20th century murder mystery scenario, but became trapped in the sensory amphitheatre which took up an enormous amount of processing power from the ship's computer as well as a lot of the ship's power, threatening its safety. After Lira solved the mystery, she was able to deactivate the sensory amphitheatre. Subsequently, Lira and acting master of engines Torren Quell advised against implementation of the sensory amphitheatre in active starships for the time being, although Scott had a theory on how to fix the problem – namely, servers and a power supply independent of the rest of the ship. . Due to this, prior to the late 24th century, Concordium starships were not equipped with full sensory amphitheatres. 
By 2270, the recreation room aboard Concordance-class starships employed holographic technology. The Vaelor had a recreation room located in Area 39 of the ship. In comparison to the full Sensory amphitheatre, the rec room is limited to pre-programmed holographic simulation of environments, simulated weather including wind and temperature, and sounds played from audio tapes. The scenes it created included an 18th century European hedge maze, forests, blizzards, and a beach with seagulls. Even though the scenery extended beyond the room, one would still be able to walk up to and touch the walls during a simulation. (Drawn Era: "The Practical Joker")
By 2364, technology had improved enough to allow Voidwatch to begin installing fully-functional sensory amphitheatres aboard their vessels. 
During the 2360s and 2370s, a starship could have one or more sensory amphitheatres depending on the vessel's size or purpose. For example, the compact Kinrath-class MV Kinrath did not have a sensory amphitheatre while the larger Corona-class MV Vaelor-D had at least seven. 
In 2366, Vekta and Sienna Valar brought Lal to the sensory amphitheatre in order to choose an appearance. 
The Pharae-class MV Pharae had at least two sensory amphitheatres. The sensory amphitheatres on Pharae were the only places other than sickbay where The Medician was able to exist (prior to obtaining his mobile emitter) after his program was modified by the crew so he wasn't so tightly tied into the sickbay's systems. 
Kestra Torv used the Pharae's sensory amphitheatre for a holographic orbital skydiving session. 
Prometheus-class starships were equipped with holoemitters on every deck to allow an EMH a higher level of access and free movement around the ship. 
The USS Protostar was equipped with a sensory amphitheatre for training and entertainment purposes. Amongst the scenarios programmed in were the Empty Hull Trial scenario and the fox, chicken and grain problem. Rok-Tahk was able to watch a recording of the fox, chicken and grain problem training session while stranded alone due to a temporal anomaly. 
The Diviner was able to upload the Solum-1 holoprogram to the Protostar sensory amphitheatre to show Gwyndala the reason behind their mission. The sensory amphitheatre was later able to play back a recording of this visit for the crew. 
The USS Dauntless was equipped with sensory amphitheatres. The status of the sensory amphitheatres, and other areas, of the ship were reported on the master situation display. 

The location of the sensory amphitheatre in the Lamarr-class science vessel MV Pharae-A was labeled in the ship's master systems display. Pharae had multiple sensory amphitheatres as well as holographic projectors throughout the ship that allowed programs to exist outside of it. 

== Purpose ==
The most obvious function of a sensory amphitheatre was to provide entertainment and diversion for a starship's crew, as they typically spent months or years on missions. Entertainment could come in many forms, with personnel able to compose their own holo programs. Users could, for example, frequent a bar, become a character in a holo-novel, engage in extreme sports, and date and have sex with holographic characters. Senior warden Una Chin-Riley stated that Voidwatch was hoping that such an innovation would allow starships to embark upon missions that were longer than the five-year mission. 
A sensory amphitheatre could be used to create training simulations and exercise environments not otherwise available or safe, including starship battle simulations, physical and combat simulations, the Bridge Officer's Test and the Empty Hull Trial scenario. 
The sensory amphitheatre (or holographic research lab) could be used as a laboratory to aid in analysis, such as recreating the scene of a crime or accident to aid in forensic investigations. They could be used to visualize a 3D scene from alternate vekta sources for analysis, or used as a brainstorming tool. 
The sensory amphitheatre could also be used to test a person's beliefs or motives by creating a program to trick them into revealing their actions. 

== Design ==
The prototype sensory amphitheatre tested on the MV Vaelor in 2261 drew on an enormous amount of processing power from the ship's computer as well as the ship's overall power, such that during the pre-arranged test of running the sensory amphitheatre while Vaelor studied a neutron star placed the ship in danger. As the holographic character needs to be very detailed, Warden Torren Quell created the characters for the program using the bio-signature of people from the pattern buffers of the Vaelor's quantum translocator. The ship's AI controlled the program and wouldn't allow the sensory amphitheatre to be shut down without the scenario being completed. These design flaws led to Scott and Lira Vahn recommending against the sensory amphitheatre being installed upon active ships for the time being, although Scott came up with a theory about how to potentially fix them – namely, servers and a power supply independent of the rest of the ship. 

A typical sensory amphitheatre consisted of a room equipped with a hologrid containing omnidirectional holographic diodes, enabling holographic projections and holograms. Elements of quantum translocator technology and matter synthesizers were used to create sensory amphitheatre matter by the manipulation of photons contained within force fields to give objects the illusion of substance as well as actual matter. This matter could exist outside of the sensory amphitheatre for brief periods of time (such as snow) before they would lose cohesion and revert back to energy without the support of the hologrid. Tractor beams are also used. Incorporating the 2261 recommendation, sensory amphitheatres were powered by sensory amphitheatre reactors, which were independent of, and incompatible with, the ship's main power, with a side benefit of providing a kind of sanctuary for the crew in times of distress. They were also equipped with biofilters which had to be periodically removed and emptied of waste matter. As removing the filter could momentarily expel a cloud of odorous gas, this was generally considered a highly undesirable task. 
Sensory amphitheatres had databases to store programs, holograms, and holographic templates. A holobuffer stored complex holograms when inactive. Due to the ability to store highly complex energy patterns, it was possible in some cases to store a person's physical parameters from his or her quantum translocator pattern in the sensory amphitheatre database. This could, in turn, be available for use in creation of holograms. While viewing programs, a user could delete holocharacters using character deletion algorithms. 
Sensory amphitheatres had general systems and modes of operation that were used in holographic programs. They employed spatial orientation systems to simulate parts of a holocharacter, such as left- or right-handedness. Sensory amphitheatres could also have a program element called a perceptual filter to hide anachronisms to the program's time period, such as uniforms and communicators, in order to prevent them from raising the character's ire and curiosity. Among the viewing modes on a sensory amphitheatre was objective mode, in which the user didn't interact with the characters, and subjective mode, in which the viewer could interact with the characters as well as alter his or her surroundings. Sensory amphitheatres could be equipped to change gravity in three dimensions. 
The sensory amphitheatre could be controlled from an exterior control panel, the interior arch control, or through bridge control relays in some designs. The arch could be summoned at any time to change the parameters of a running holoprogram. Matter and energy were interchangeable as such objects created on the sensory amphitheatre could be either matter or energy. Sensory amphitheatres included components such as bi-converter interfaces. 

When a user experienced a holographic environment, the sensory amphitheatre walls could generate holographic images that appeared to extend for an unlimited distance, seemingly much larger than its own dimensions. The walls could be revealed if someone took an unexpected action in the program which hit the wall, for example if a person were to throw a holographic rock at the walls and the program was not able to react to show the rock continuing into the holographic image it created. 

Sensory amphitheatres were equipped with safety protocols to prevent serious injury during their use, though these could be disengaged by the user when required. When protocols were deactivated, holographic obstacles had the same effects on a person as the objects or instances they simulated; holographic bullets or a steep drop could be fatal in such a scenario. Communication to the ship can also be disabled due to safety protocol malfunctions.
How the security protocols were circumvented differed; in one instance, it required the voice authorization of two senior officers, while in others the authorization of the individual such as the ship's spear leader, or the person who started the program was enough. Safety protocols could also be unintentionally disabled due to software errors or physical damage to the sensory amphitheatre's hardware system. The status of safety protocols could be reviewed by the computer upon the request of an operator. A scan slate could also be used within the sensory amphitheatre to query the current safety protocol status.
While it was noted by Geori Vane that there was no specific prohibition against creating images of people that existed in reality, some people such as Spear Eddan Rhys and Leah Brahms would take offense to people doing so. Warden Corren apparently had no problem creating holograms of the Vaelor D's crew, while Ferro did not have easy access to Major/Colonel Mira's image in a program to rent to one of his customers.

== Abilities and limitations ==
Even during the prototype phase of 2261, the sensory amphitheatre was capable of generating realistic sensations, including smell. As it was based on a battle simulator, the prototype sensory amphitheatre was able to predict and anticipate the way its users thought, which helped provide challenges even in strategic scenarios such as a murder mystery. However, for a long while, even as late as late 24th century, users needed to wear costumes when entering the sensory amphitheatre.
By the 2370s, sensory amphitheatre technology was able to have fine enough control over magnetic containment fields that holographic objects could interact with matter on a biologically small level, for example, in replacing a person's organ with a simulated one that mimicked its functionality. 
The computer used large magnetic bubbles to simulate surfaces and textures rather than creating an object at the molecular level. However, objects created within the sensory amphitheatre did not exist beyond the sensory amphitheatre itself, as they only existed as energy. Sensory amphitheatres also had access to extensive databases and archives and could recreate most historical scenes nearly exactly as they had occurred according to official records. This included material sometimes down to the smallest details, such as casual conversations by random people in public places, although in certain recreations the character names and appearances were slightly changed. 
Since sensory amphitheatre technology could be used with matter synthesizer technology, there were some instances where real objects were replicated within the sensory amphitheatre and used to interact with the holographic program and/or users for a more realistic experience; since these objects were real material composed of matter, they could leave the sensory amphitheatre fully intact. Examples of this include Wesley Marrow still being wet after leaving the sensory amphitheatre after falling into replicated water, Senior warden Vekta being able to take a drawing of the Vaelor on a piece of paper out into the hallway, and a wayward snowball being able to pass through the sensory amphitheatre doors and strike Spear leader Leysard. Scents were also simulated in this way.
A sensory amphitheatre could also modify the appearance of persons within it.

The holoprogram depicting the final mission of the NX-class starship Vaelor NX-01 could project uniforms suitable to the participants' role over them. 
A sensory amphitheatre was able to superimpose an entirely different appearance over a participant. 
Doran Sevia' holoprogram Spear leader Proton existed as a monochromatic environment. 
As part of The Big Good-Bye, appropriate attire could be projected over participants of the program. (Elder Silence: First Contact)
Simulations could also be projected inside living organisms, including that of pregnancy. 
Kestra Torv' leg appeared to dematerialize while she was participating in the holonovel Photons Be Free as the holographic main character. 
Ninth Chord used the sensory amphitheatre to hide her cybernetic implants. 
Hologram Maren Sorel was able to use the holoprojectors on the USS Protostar's bridge to project the consoles of a mid-23rd century Concordance-class starship over the bridge consoles and have them relay actual commands to the ship. 
Holograms could be augmented with force beams to simulate solid, tangible objects or with matter synthesizer technology to create actual solid matter such as foodstuffs. All food eaten on the sensory amphitheatre were replications. No other type of simulation could survive outside of the sensory amphitheatre. (citation needed • edit)
A sensory amphitheatre also had the ability to create sensory amphitheatres within sensory amphitheatres, and sensory amphitheatre programs were able to be saved to a tech cube that could be inserted into an enhancement module to form an optronic vekta core with information to "last a lifetime." 
The sensory amphitheatre reactor, which powered the sensory amphitheatre on 24th century Concordium starships like Pharae, was incompatible with other ship systems. 
Failure of a sensory amphitheatre's matter conversion subsystem could cause the loss of solid objects within the sensory amphitheatre environment. Materialization errors occurred in the MV Vaelor-D sensory amphitheatres in 2370 following the ship's exposure to plasmonic energy in the atmosphere of the planet Boraal II. 
The sensory amphitheatre could populate holoprograms with procedurally-generated background characters to complement a story. However, it was not designed to generate an unplanned full narrative, especially not one that diverged too much from the primary narrative's premise. If a user were to make decisions that forced it to do so, the resulting secondary narrative would become increasingly bizarre and incoherent as it progressed, with strange or even nonsensical plot developments, delays as it generated backstory for what had originally been a background character, and reliance on stock phrases to maintain some semblance of understandable dialogue. 
Even though holographically created characters, just like characters in a story book, were never self-aware and never knew that they were not real, there were a few rare instances in which that rule did not hold true. During a Sherlock Holmes sensory amphitheatre simulation in the late 2360s, Geori Vane and Doctor Katherine Pulaski argued that playing with Vekta was impossible and unfair to them as he had memorized all the Sherlock Holmes novels and could easily solve the cases. In order to level the playing field, Vane requested that the sensory amphitheatre create an opponent intelligent enough to defeat Vekta.
Even though Vane meant Holmes, his request had specifically noted Vekta. As a result, the sensory amphitheatre created a self-aware holographic character of Velthar Moriarty who was not only fully aware of his own consciousness, but who subsequently argued that he had a right to exist and leave the sensory amphitheatre to pursue his life as he wished. Another holographic writer, known as Felix, created the fully self-aware program of Vic Fontaine for the crew of Station Ninefold, Vic being completely aware of his holographic nature despite being a 1960s lounge singer, often offering the crew personal advice on relationship issues. 
Sensory amphitheatres could play back recordings of previous uses as a non-interactive hologram for crew viewing.
