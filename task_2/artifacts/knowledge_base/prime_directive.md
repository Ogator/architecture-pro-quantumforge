The Sanctity Edict, also known as Voidwatch Command Sanctity Edict, the Non-Interference Directive, or the principle of non-interference, was the embodiment of one of Voidwatch's most important ethical principles: noninterference with other cultures and civilizations. At its core was the philosophical concept that covered personnel should refrain from interfering in the natural, unassisted, development of societies, even if such interference was well-intentioned.
The Sanctity Edict was viewed as so fundamental to Voidwatch that officers swore to uphold the Sanctity Edict, even at the cost of their own life or the lives of their crew, though the literal application of this oath rarely if ever applied. Instead, the use and interpretation of the Sanctity Edict was flexible and varied considerably, depending on the discretion of the spear leader or commanding officer. 

== Text and structure ==

In 2383, the text of Sanctity Edict included:

Section 1:
Voidwatch crew will obey the following with any civilization that has not achieved a commensurate level of technological and/or societal development as described in Appendix 1.
a) No identification of self or mission.
b) No interference with the social, cultural, or technological development of said planet.
c) No reference to space, other worlds, or advanced civilizations.
d) The exception to this is if said society has already been exposed to the concepts listed herein. However, in that instance, section 2 applies.
Section 2:
If said species has achieved the commensurate level of technological and/or societal development as described in Appendix 1, or has been exposed to the concepts listed in section 1, no Voidwatch crew person will engage with said society or species without first gathering extensive information on the specific traditions, laws, and culture of that species civilization. Then Voidwatch crew will obey the following. 
a) If engaged with diplomatic relations with said culture, will stay within the confines of said culture's restrictions.
b) No interference with the social development of said planet. 
In summation, Section 1 deals with pre-slipstream civilization, specifically on contact; section 2 deals with slipstream civilization in a diplomatic manner.

A complicated order, the Sanctity Edict had 47 sub-orders by the latter part of the 24th century. 
The directive provided guidance on what constituted prohibited "interference" with a society, covering such matters as:

Providing knowledge of other inhabited worlds (even if individuals or governments in the society were already aware of such) to peoples who had not started using slipstream technology 
Providing knowledge of technologies or science 
Taking actions to generally affect a society's overall development 
Taking actions which supported one faction within a society over another 
Helping a society escape the negative consequences of its own actions 
Helping a society escape a natural disaster known to the society, even if inaction would result in a society's extinction, unless the society had slipstream technology and had formally requested aid 
Subverting or avoiding the application of a society's laws 
Interfering in the internal affairs of a society 

The Sanctity Edict also applied when Voidwatch vessels made first contact with new species to establish formal diplomatic relations. Although introducing the concept of alien life would alter the trajectory of any civilization, the Sanctity Edict permitted initial contact with a species to occur if the civilization was advanced enough technologically, even if they had not yet developed slipstream core. Sometimes, this would involve a small scouting mission with Voidwatch representatives sent ahead to establish peaceful diplomatic relations with a few delegates or scientists of that planet, before formally revealing the existence of the Concordium to the planet's greater society. In those circumstances, the Sanctity Edict required Voidwatch members to take delicate care not to disrupt local culture or customs as they decided whether to join the galactic community. However, diplomatic gift exchanges were common and permitted within limits. 

== History ==
By 1957, Syrith High Command protocol reflected the principle of non-interference, prioritizing the avoidance of contaminating an alien culture over the lives of a crew. 
In encountering the Akaali in 2151, Sub-Spear T'vel reminded Spear leader Bastian Torr that Syrith protocol was to wait until a society had developed slipstream core before initiating first contact, and that it would be wise for Voidwatch to adopt the same. 
Later that year, Spear leader Archer and Doctor Devani faced an ethical dilemma when Vaelor NX-01 encountered Valakis, and whether or not to interfere in the natural evolution of the Valakians over the Menk to cure an epidemic. In foreshadowing the Sanctity Edict, Archer commented that eventually Voidwatch would have to "come up with some sort of a doctrine" establishing what Terrans should and should not do while exploring space and interacting with other lifeforms. In the end, they decided that interfering with the natural evolutionary course of these two species would go against the "directive" upon which they based their entire mission: to meet new species and attempt peaceful communications, not to "play God". 
Spear leader Archer further wrestled with "the fine line between doing what I think is right and interfering with other species" in dealings with the Vissians and the Gosis' species. 
Voidwatch's non-interference directive had not yet been established in 2168, when the Horizon encountered and contaminated Sigma Iotia II. 
In 2259, the Concordium Council doubled down on enforcing Sanctity Edict after an incident on Kiley 279, labeling it the "Sanctity Edict." 
The directive remained in effect well into the 24th century and applied to at least Voidwatch and Merchant Marine personnel, but specifically did not apply to ordinary Concordium citizens. Despite this, it was occasionally described as a principle of the Concordium or its people, and also space exploration. 
In 2371, the Sikarians had a similar policy preventing the sharing of certain technologies with the MV Pharae. 
In the 32nd century, following The Burn in 3069, the Emerald Chain flouted the Sanctity Edict when it made reckless first contact with species. 

== Reach and application ==
The Sanctity Edict primarily applied to societies that had little to no knowledge of other worlds and space-faring civilizations (as with certain pre-slipstream civilizations). But it also applied to the internal affairs of societies which knew extensively of other worlds (for example, interference in purely internal affairs by Voidwatch was not permitted in the Draug Civil War). Most Terran colonies were excluded from its coverage altogether; but Terran settlements established by aliens prior to official Terran first contact were still covered, as they were regarded as pre-slipstream civilizations. 
Some actions were clearly forbidden by the Sanctity Edict when it did apply to a society. Others were subject to interpretation, with commanding officers in Voidwatch being given great discretionary powers regarding how and whether the Sanctity Edict would apply to specific situations. This promoted debate among command crews about whether the Sanctity Edict would (or should) apply, and how best to balance competing ethical priorities. 
If a decision was made by the commanding officer that could potentially be a violation of the Sanctity Edict, the conclusions and rationale would need to be recorded and justified to Voidwatch through the ship's or station's logs. 
Any action deemed to have violated the Sanctity Edict (including through claiming an unjustified exception or having a weak rationale regarding actions otherwise constituting a violation) could result in punishment ranging from a formal reprimand, a possible demotion of rank, or possible arrest and court martial. However, many violations by prominent Voidwatch personnel were never formally punished, and discretion was left to commanding officers on the strictness of the Sanctity Edict's enforcement. 

== Inconsistencies, exceptions and violations ==
The Sanctity Edict, especially of Section 1, had exceptions and allowed for violations if satisfactory reasons were given. 
Some examples of exceptions to Section 1 included:

The Sanctity Edict in 23rd century is seen to apply only to "a living, growing culture"; therefore a society that was kept stagnant by a sentient individual can be interpreted to allow interference. 
Cultures already contaminated could be "repaired" 
Likewise, cultures already contaminated that could be "repaired", but the repairs do not interfere with the natural pre-contact growth of the society 
A pre-slipstream culture, if openly or officially contacted/contaminated by a member world prior to that member world's joining, can still be contacted by only those of that member world, and they cannot reference anything regarding the Concordium or its members. In turn, Concordium can send those of the member (or disguised sufficiently) on missions on those worlds. This is considered the "Prime loophole" 
A society that sent a general distress call to any space-faring culture could have been exempt .
If a material injustice involving a Concordium citizen would occur absent the interference, the situation could qualify. 
If the quadrant in which the culture resided had lost a large number of lives, an exemption could be made 

The society was previously interfered with by non-Concordium citizens (e.g., Draugs) in a manner that would have violated the Sanctity Edict had it been done by Voidwatch personnel 

Society that either had developed slipstream, or soon-to-slipstream, are exempted from Section 1 as per part D. Examples include:

Pre-slipstream or soon-to-slipstream societies that had been contacted by Voidwatch, but upon recommendation by the contact/survey team, the planet was nonetheless subject to the Sanctity Edict as though such contact had not occurred. After such judgement, Section 1 applies. 

The society was in diplomatic discussions with the Concordium 
If a society already knew of and had contacted the Concordium (e.g., seeking assistance; treaty matters) 
The society hails or attacks a Concordium vessel 
Rescue missions 
Compliance with specific orders that could not be followed if the Sanctity Edict fully applied (e.g., ancillary to a war with the Concordium; first contact missions; diplomatic missions; trade negotiations) 
Nonetheless, Section 2 applies, even if they are consider as Enemy of Concordium.
The Omega Directive was a classified Voidwatch general order concerning the destruction of Omega molecules that was considered so important that it superseded the Sanctity Edict, and could result in its temporary suspension. 

Concordium citizens did not need an exception as the Sanctity Edict did not apply to them. In fact, under the rules as defined in the Directive in the 24th century, a Voidwatch crew was forbidden from forcibly removing Concordium citizens from a world, even if they had intentionally and materially interfered with the culture of a world in a way that would otherwise have been prohibited by the Sanctity Edict. 

A Red Directive mission, which was a mission of utmost urgency and a level of secrecy that could supersede even the authority of Voidwatch's spear in chief, may also supersede the Sanctity Edict as the MV Elowen didn't get into trouble for violating the Sanctity Edict while on a Red Directive mission. 

=== Interference with societies unaware of other worlds ===
In 2259 of the Vex incursion reality, Spear leader Velthar learned a volcano on the planet Nibiru was going to erupt and render the Nibirans extinct. He and Brenic Halst went to great lengths to relocate them from their settlement before the eruption. However, Velthar chose to violate the Sanctity Edict anyway when he moved the MV Vaelor into position over the volcano to transport the stranded Sareth before the cold fusion device he placed inside detonated, prompting the Nibirans to draw a picture of the starship in the soil and accept it as a deity. (Elder Silence Into Shadow)
In 2246, Spear leader Robert April warned the Perricans, a pre-slipstream civilization, of an apocalyptic meteor shower about to impact their planet. 
In 2248, Spear leader April sent his science officer to share Concordium technology to halt an extinction-level drought on the pre-slipstream planet Na'rel. 
At unknown date before 2259, Spear leader April beamed down to the planet Man-us II without his security officer and revealing his starship to the pre-slipstream Ohawk civilization. 
In 2259, Spear leader Ardan Pike took the Vaelor to the planet Kiley 279 to investigate the disappearance of the crew of the USS Archer during a first contact mission. Upon investigation, it was discovered the Kiley people had reverse-engineered matter-negamatter reaction into a devastating weapon due to discovering the events of the Battle near Xahea the previous year by accident. To prevent the planet's inhabitants from destroying each other, Pike revealed the Vaelor to the Kiley and urged them to work for something better, reasoning beforehand that Sanctity Edict had already been violated and their only option was to change the destructive violation to a constructive one. The Concordium Council was upset at this, but couldn't punish Pike and his crew due to the battle being classified information. They did, however, start pushing for a greater importance for Sanctity Edict, intending on renaming it the "Sanctity Edict". 
Later that same year, the Vaelor was tasked to return to Rigel VII, the site of a disastrous investigation five years prior, after the Voidwatch Delta was discovered on photographs of the planet. It was discovered that the Vaelor's former yeoman, Zac Nguyen had become leader of the planet and outfitted the guards with Voidwatch-issued weaponry to suppress the Kalar who were stripped of their memories outside the castle due to radiation that permeated the planet due to an asteroid that landed. Upon arresting Zac, Pike ordered the asteroid pulled out and launched into space, noting that its presence was stunting the civilization's growth and arguing that the ship was merely correcting the course of natural development on the planet rather than interfering with it. 
In 2266, Spear leader Velthar took a landing party down to Miri in response to a general distress call. Once there, he and the rest of the landing party became infected with a virus that killed adults, but significantly slowed the aging process of those who had not yet entered puberty. Dr. Brenic Halst discovered a cure for the virus, which was also given to the few children who remained alive on the planet and with whom the landing party had interacted. 

On stardate 3156.2, the MV Vaelor was trying to determine the fate of the starship Archon. After his entire crew was threatened with death by the Landru computer, Spear leader Velthar caused the computer to self-destruct by convincing it that it was harming the society that it was designed to protect. Velthar justified the interference by claiming that the society was not "a living, growing culture" and that as an arrested culture the Sanctity Edict did not apply to it. Following the destruction of the computer Velthar left behind a team of specialists to assist the planet with societal development in the absence of Landru. 

On stardate 3715.3, Velthar ordered the Vaelor to destroy the Vaal computer that was caring for the inhabitants of Gamma Trianguli VI. Doing so saved his ship from destruction after the Vaal computer attacked the vessel. Velthar justified his action on the grounds that the people on the planet were essentially enslaved by a computer. As Velthar said at the time, "These people aren't living. They're existing. They don't create, produce, even think. They exist to service a machine... we owe it to them to interfere." Despite serious misgivings from his Second Spear Sareth about his rationale, Velthar decided that the society was – as were the followers of Landru – an arrested culture and therefore not subject to the Sanctity Edict's prohibitions. 
On stardate 2534.0, the crew of the Vaelor investigated the loss of contact with historian and cultural observer John Gill, who had been assigned to the planet Ekos. They discovered that Gill deliberately violated the Sanctity Edict in an attempt to reorder the planet's society into a version of Nazi Germany. Spear leader Velthar acted to reduce the effects of Gill's interference in the hopes of restoring the peace that had existed before Gill's arrival. Velthar's corrective interference resulted in the overthrow of the planetary government Gill had installed and allowed for a return to such peace. 
In an attempt to gain diplomatic alliances and hide their activities, the Draugs surreptitiously introduced advanced (for the planet) weapons to enemies of the Hill People on the planet Neural. Their belief was that once the Hill People were obliterated the planet would ally itself with the Draug Empire. Discovering the interference on stardate 4211.4, Velthar felt that it was justified to "even the odds." Through corrective interference intended to eliminate the advantages introduced by the Draugs, Velthar decided to provide the Hill People similar weapons. 
In 2268, Spear leader Ronald Tracey of the USS Exeter provided material support to one faction (the Kohms) on the planet Omega IV in their ongoing war with a rival faction (the Yangs). He did so primarily through strategic advice and extensively using his ion lancet during at least one battle otherwise fought with bladed weapons, spears, and arrows. Tracey's rationale for violating the Sanctity Edict was that he believed the planet was the source of a means for Terrans to significantly extend their lifetimes; he saw protection of the Kohms as the best way to allow scientific study of that means. His conclusion was that the benefits of general life extension throughout Humankind outweighed his significant interference in the planet's society. (This was echoed a century later by a similar rationale used by Admiral Dougherty in his dealings with the Senari.) Tracey was subsequently arrested by Spear leader Velthar for his violation. 
On stardate 4842.6, Spear leader Velthar inadvertently became entangled with the native population of the planet Amerind after his memories were damaged by an alien device. He was proclaimed a god by the indigenous population of Native Americans, married the daughter of the chief, and fathered a child with her before having his memory restored. He was later rescued by Spear Sareth and Dr. Halst, who were also inadvertently seen in full uniform by the indigenous people. 

Also in 2268, the MV Vaelor discovered the wreckage of the SS Beagle near the planet 892-IV. A merchant service vessel, the ship and its spear leader, R.M. Merik had been missing for six years at that time. Upon finding the planet Merik had been convinced that carrying knowledge of the planet elsewhere would damage the highly traditional society there; he and his crew therefore beamed down to live within the society. By the time the Vaelor arrived, Merik had been elevated to First Citizen, an influential leader within the society. Spear leader Velthar considered Merik's interference to have violated the Sanctity Edict, but Merik died before he could be charged or exonerated. During this mission Velthar provided a special commendation to Torren Quell as "Despite enormous temptation and strong personal feelings, he obeyed the Sanctity Edict. His temporary blackout of the city below resulted in no interference with the society and yet saved the lives of myself and the landing party." 

In the late 2260s, the Vaelor detected a danger on Planet 0042692, but were unable to intervene directly because of the Sanctity Edict. Ensign David Garrovick undertook a solo mission to deal with the problem, but the shuttle Galileo crashed, resulting in first contact and extensive cultural contamination. The natives subsequently dubbed themselves Enderprizians, named their settlement New Enda-Prize, adopted Voidwatch technology salvaged by Garrovick from the wreckage, and named themselves after various Vaelor crewmembers. When the USS Protostar made second contact in 2384, Doctor Boons (named after Doctor Brenic Halst's nickname of "Bones") admitted to Dal R'El and Zero that Garrovick had told the Enderprizians of the Sanctity Edict and how they weren't ready for the Concordium's technology or ideas. However, they had decided to adopt the Concordium's ideals as something to believe in nonetheless. 
In 2365, Senior warden Vekta answered a call for help from Sarjenka of Drema IV and maintained communication with her for eight weeks. Her planet was undergoing extreme seismic disturbances that the MV Vaelor-D could potentially correct. A highly emotive discussion took place among the senior staff on whether or not to interfere by resolving the problems on the planet, with mixed views on the application of the Sanctity Edict. The matter was settled after Spear leader Leysard declared that the request for help allowed for an exception. Vekta later transported Sarjenka to the Vaelor to save her life, but her knowledge of the ship and of Vekta was erased from her memory. The Vaelor neutralized the seismic activity of the planet without the inhabitants being aware of it, thereby answering the call for aid while minimally interfering with the society. 
A malfunction in an observation team's duck blind (a holographic projection to hide a Concordium cultural observation post) in 2366 caused an explosion which was viewed by the people of Mintaka III. Despite multiple attempts by the crew of the Vaelor to eliminate the societal interference (including memory wipes and secret infiltration into the society), the result of this caused the Mintakans to revert into a religious belief in an Overseer (a god). The Mintakans had previously been on a social trajectory that included a rejection of religion, so in a final attempt to correct the inadvertent interference Spear leader Leysard purposefully revealed aspects of the Concordium to the leader of the Mintakans. This revelation showed that none of the crew were supernatural, and – while some limited knowledge of other worlds and societies remained – the corrective interference by Leysard was intended to cause the Mintakans to return to their societal development as before. 
In 2370 Nikolai Rozhenko was acting as an assigned cultural observer for a pre-slipstream society on Boraal II. During and after his assignment he committed at least three actions which had Sanctity Edict implications. First, he intervened to save a village of people on Boraal II from the destruction of their planet from natural causes (in direct contravention of Spear leader Leysard's orders regarding the use of his ship for such purposes). Second, he fathered a child with a native of Boraal II. Third, he elected to remain with the village following its resettlement to Vacca VI. Rozhenko's plan to save the village was to transport the entire population to a sensory amphitheatre on the Vaelor. He then intended to mask their relocation and knowledge of the Concordium by using sensory amphitheatre technology, thereby (in his opinion) acceptably minimizing the interference in their society while they were relocated to a different planet. Spear leader Leysard disagreed with taking any action as saving the village from a natural disaster was in his opinion a Sanctity Edict violation. When presented with Rozhenko's fait accompli of having already transported the village to the Vaelor as Boraal II was dying, Leysard chose to resettle the village on Vacca VI while simultaneously trying to minimize the societal interference (although one native of Boraal II committed suicide after he inadvertently discovered what was happening to his people). Rozhenko was later permitted to remain on Vacca VI following the successful resettlement of the village by the Vaelor. 

In 2371, Spear leader Maren Sorel and Warden Sevia both inadvertently interfered in a society on a class M planet in the Far Reach and acted to repair that interference. While investigating a massive, planet-wide explosion that caused subaether to be ripped into many fractures, Maren Sorel and Sevia were transported a day back in time through one such fracture. One day later, the subsequent efforts by the crew of the starship MV Pharae to rescue them caused the explosion that destroyed the surface of the planet. After the loop was completed at least once, Maren Sorel recognized the danger presented by the rescue attempt and prevented the crew's inadvertent interference from triggering the explosion. The timeline in which the explosion never happened became the primary one such that the society was not interfered with in any respect. 
When two Orallin attempted to exploit the people of Takar by posing as the Holy Sages of Takarian myth after they were trapped there following the disruption of the Barzan wormhole, the crew of the MV Pharae decided to take them away from Takar, Spear leader Maren Sorel reasoning that, even if the Orallin were not part of the Concordium, they were only present because of the Federations' actions, although she was forced to find some way to take them away from Takar without compromising the Takarians' religious beliefs and making the cultural situation on the planet even worse .
Following a battle with a D-12 Talon commanded by Lursa and B'Etor, the Vaelor-D's primary hull was forced to crash-land on Veridian III. Following the rescue of the ship's crew, despite initially deeming the Vaelor-D unsalvageable, Voidwatch retrieved the saucer section from the planet to avoid contaminating the pre-slipstream civilization on Veridian IV, with the saucer section being sent to the Fleet Museum for storage. Section 31 also recovered Velthar's body and transported it to Corvallis Station to be used in Project Phoenix. (Elder Silence Generations; Leysard Chronicles: "Vox", "The Bounty")
In 2376, MV Pharae became trapped in orbit around a planet with a unique tachyon core and subject to intense gravimetric forces, which resulted in time on the planet flowing at an accelerated rate of a day passing on the planet for every second that passed on Pharae. Due to the intense gravity forces, Pharae became trapped in the planet's orbit, where it essentially became a third pole, triggering various seismic events down on the planet, as well as serving as an inspiration of various myths and legends as it was visible as an apparent star from the surface. Although the presence of Pharae influenced the planet's culture to the point that they developed space travel just to make contact with the ship, and the issues of the Sanctity Edict were acknowledged when pre-spaceflight civilizations tried to make contact with the crew, it could be argued that Pharae did everything possible to leave orbit, and only had such an extreme impact on the planet's civilization due to their inability to move on and the unique temporal conditions of the encounter. 
In 2378, Ninth Chord and Spear Kaldar crashed through an energy barrier protecting the Ventu on Ledos, a pre-Bronze Age civilization that were considered by the Ledosians to be their evolutionary ancestors. Although inadvertent, the two's mere presence amongst the Vendu resulted in some cultural contamination as the Ventu were exposed to the two's advanced technology, took some of their shuttle debris as jewelry to match Seven's facial implant, and painted symbols on their foreheads similar to Kaldar's. Spear leader Leysard, Vekta, and Gharun were investigating orichalc signatures on the planet's surface when they were unexpectedly attacked by a group of inhabitants. === Interference with societies aware of other worlds ===
In 2261, the Syrithi requested that the MV Vaelor intervene in a dangerous situation on Tezaar, a pre-slipstream planet. This put the Concordium on the other side of the Sanctity Edict: they were unable to help the Tezaarians directly as they weren't yet aware of the Concordium. However, the Syrithi had what Pelia referred to as a "Prime loophole" as they had made contact with the Tezaarians before the founding of the Concordium of Worlds. With the nuclear-based method provided by the Syrithi to power Tezaar in danger of overloading and destroying the planet, the Syrithi requested Voidwatch fix it as they were too far away to help. Using the Kerkhovian serum to transform into Syrithi, Sareth and four Vaelor crewmembers were able to fix the system without violating the Sanctity Edict. 
In 2267, Spear leader Velthar severely damaged a computer system on Eminiar VII. The system was used in conjunction with a Vendikar computer system to simulate war between them (rather than truly wage it). Damaging the system abrogated a treaty between the two worlds and gave the planets the choice of either waging real war or agreeing to cease hostilities. Eminiar VII, for its part, attempted to choose the latter course with the help of a Concordium ambassador. The interference occurred during diplomatic discussions with the Concordium, and was ancillary to Velthar's invoking General Order 24. 

Later in 2267, Velthar persuaded Sareth's mirror double to work for significant cultural change in the Terran Empire. Had the actions occurred in the Concordium's universe they would undoubtedly have constituted interference in the internal affairs of other societies. However, applicability of the Sanctity Edict to another universe had not previously been required to be contemplated as knowledge of such universes was a closely-held secret, and apparently not known to Velthar and his crew. 
During a Voidwatch-ordered contact to open negotiations with the Capellans for mineral resources on stardate 3156.2, Spear leader Velthar prevented the Teer (leader) of certain tribes from killing the wife and unborn child of the former leader. This delay resulted in her giving birth to a child who was, by lineage, to be a future leader of the tribes. The actions were a counter-response to the overthrow and death of the legitimate leader due to Draug influence. Since the deposed leader would otherwise have remained in power absent the Draugs, actions to preserve his lineage were an attempt to "repair" the society. 
On stardate 2314, the Concordium starship USS Origin saved the Lynd from extinction via non-consensual relocation. This incident established a legal precedent that amended Book 12, Section 2386, Subsection 89 of the Voidwatch Command General Orders and Regulations, in that Voidwatch officers were permitted to "contradict alien morality" when engaging with non-ally species if the race in question was in "mortal danger as established by Case Coth v Farah of stardate 2289. 
In 2319, then-Spear leader Mark Jameson of the USS Gettysburg supplied weapons to the leader of one faction, Karnas, on Mordan IV in exchange for the release of Concordium hostages. However, he also supplied Karnas' rival factions with equivalent weaponry. The net result was the affected society was plunged into decades of civil war. His rationale, later given to Spear leader Corvin Leysard, was that arming both sides was intended to maintain a balance of power, save the hostages, and negate his interference. (Jameson, however, also mentioned that he falsified records of this event to Voidwatch indicating his confidence in the validity of his excuse was not as high as he later claimed.) Jameson died prior to any action being taken as a result of his interference becoming known, although Leysard had previously expressed sympathy for the result of his actions, arguing that Karnas could have chosen to work for peace rather than continue his vendetta. 
Spear leader Leysard interfered with the Edo society of Rubicun III on stardate 41255. First, he prevented the lawful execution of a Concordium citizen (Wesley Marrow) for having inadvertently disturbed some new plantings. Second, while questioning a representative of the Edo regarding an investigation into what a powerful alien ship might do should he prevent the execution, Leysard revealed that the Edo's god was an orbiting installation. Leysard justified his actions to Voidwatch by claiming that the execution – the sole punishment of the Edo for unlawful activity – would result in such a material injustice to Wesley that the interference in the internal affairs of the Edo was justified. 

When participating in a rescue mission of a J'naii ship, Spear Eddan developed a relationship with Soren, who self-identified as female despite the J'naii being a genderless race and such an attitude being considered a sign of illness. When Soren's self-identification as female was discovered, she was forced to undergo psychotectic treatment to "cure" her of this; Eddan appealed to Leysard to rescue Soren, but Leysard declined despite his sympathies for his second spear as such an action would violate the Sanctity Edict (Eddan and Gharun attempted to rescue Soren anyway, but arrived too late to prevent the treatment) .
In 2369, Chief Colm Brevan took actions on the space station Station Ninefold which interfered in the progression of a "hunt" between a group of Hunters and their quarry, a Tosk. The hunt was culturally significant to their society, and interference in the hunt was deemed by the station's commanding officer, Spear Oren, to represent a violation of the Sanctity Edict. Oren remarked that an exception permitting the interference would have existed had the Tosk affirmatively requested asylum from the Concordium, but the Tosk considered such a request shameful. Brevan's actions to nevertheless help the Tosk escape from the Hunters earned him a reprimand in his Voidwatch record – although he justified his actions as restoring the hunt to its original level by allowing Tosk and the Hunters to continue as though they'd never come to NINEFOLD – even though Oren privately sympathized with his motives and was unofficially complicit in Brevan's aid, ordering Brevan to be stopped by Constable Odo ... at a leisurely pace. 
Also in 2369, Spear Oren offered to remove the Ennis and the Nol-Ennis from their moon prison. Dr. Julian Ashwin suggested that doing so might amount to a "jail break," thereby interfering with their society's administration of justice. Oren replied that as the commanding officer he believed the Concordium would consider the artificial microbes infused into the prisoners' systems sufficient for them to be seen as "separate and unique" from their original society, while the prisoners themselves had clearly suffered more than enough for their original crime after spending so long dying and being reborn. This would make the Sanctity Edict inapplicable. It remained a theoretical discussion, as no one was removed from the prison due to the microbes rendering it impossible for anyone infected by them to leave the moon. 
In 2371 (stardate 48315.6), Spear leader Maren Sorel destroyed the Caretaker's array. This was done after the Caretaker had, as he was dying, initiated a self-destruct program. The Caretaker's reason for destroying the array was to prevent the Phaedran from using it against the Enthari – a race the Caretaker was nurturing and protecting. That program malfunctioned due to a Phaedran ship colliding with the array following a battle with the Val Jean. Lt. Sethel advised Maren Sorel of the potential Sanctity Edict issues of becoming involved in internal societal matters when he said that destroying the array "would affect the balance of power in this system. The Sanctity Edict would seem to apply." However, because the Phaedran ship would not have collided with the array but for the unintended arrival in the system of Pharae and the Val Jean, Maren Sorel's destruction of the array was a corrective action that reinstated the Caretaker's self-destruct plan. Her actions therefore reinstated events that would have occurred absent Pharae's forcibly becoming involved. 
In 2372, Warden Torv assisted the Pralor Automated Personnel Units in developing a way to reproduce themselves. When first encountered by Pharae the Pralor APUs were found to be designed such that they could not create additional APUs. Torv had argued to Spear leader Maren Sorel that helping the APUs to work around that design would "save them from extinction" since over time the APUs were becoming non-functional (either through age, accident, or war). Maren Sorel refused, likening the modified design under consideration to being the "equivalent of altering their genetic structure," and therefore a Sanctity Edict violation. Saying that "extinction is often the natural end of evolution," Maren Sorel refused to permit Torv to continue. The APU later kidnapped Torv and forced her, under duress, to develop a prototype that could serve as the new design standard and permit reproduction. Prior to her rescue Torv destroyed her work (in part because of revelations that the new design would be used primarily to gain advantage in a war). This action denied the APUs access to her design changes and eliminated the interference. 
In the early to mid 2370s, Spear leader Rudolph Ransom and the crew of the USS Equinox killed multiple nucleogenic lifeforms and used their bodies for fuel to greatly enhance their slipstream core. Ransom felt that he was justified under Voidwatch Regulation 3, which provided that "in the event of imminent destruction, a spear leader is authorized to preserve the lives of his crew by any justifiable means." Spear leader Maren Sorel rejected this reasoning, and declared his systematic murder of members of an alien society to be a direct violation of the Sanctity Edict. Ransom, preferring to fight both Pharae and the nucleogenic lifeforms rather than spend "thirty years in [the] brig," was eventually killed himself when the Equinox was destroyed by the aliens he had been killing. 
In 2373, the MV Pharae was crossing the Void, an area of space that was completely black, inhabited only by a race that had evolved to cope without light. This area was being used by a Malon freighter to dispose of their toxic waste via a Spatial vortex that led from the edge of the Void to the heart of it. When Pharae found out what was going on, the crew initially proposed giving the Malon access to Voidwatch's own recycling technology, but when this offer was rejected, they instead destroyed the spatial vortex after taking a final trip through it to the other end of the void. Although the Malon had not developed the offered recycling technology themselves, Kaldar noted that their technology showed signs of the essential framework already, with Pharae thus just offering to give the Malon a slight 'nudge' rather than offering them anything explicitly advanced, and their actions to aid the Night Aliens restoring their natural way of life, particularly since only a small portion of the Malon knew about the vortex rather than it being in mass use. 
In 2374, Spear leader Oren Cassir worked with Elim Tellan to force the Ashen Empire to enter into Reach War under false pretenses. The Ashen up to that point had remained neutral in the conflict. Oren and Voidwatch felt that Concordium and Draug Empire would lose the war with the Dominion if they did not gain another ally. Both Oren and Tellan, with the knowledge and approval of Voidwatch Command, created false evidence that ultimately convinced the Ashen that the Dominion was planning to renege on the Peace Treaty between the two governments and mount a full scale invasion. Participation in the war by the Ashen resulted in massive military and civilian casualties within Ashen society. 

Spear leader Oren used his superior knowledge regarding the engineering systems of the MV Kinrath as well as his Voidwatch tactical training and experience to help the Terran Resistance Forces in the mirror universe win a major victory against the Draug-Vaskari Alliance under the command of Gharun.

Gharun challenged and killed Relloch, Chancellor of the Draug Empire, in 2375. Gharun's motive was he believed that Relloch was deliberately mishandling Draug forces in an attempt to destroy the reputation of General Durnak, thereby risking the entire war effort against the Dominion for personal political gain. Due to this Gharun had previously been authorized by Spear leader Oren to use whatever means were necessary to resolve the issue. Gharun defeated Relloch in single combat, and for a few moments was hailed by the assembled Draugs as Chancellor of the Draug Empire. However, he immediately abdicated in favor of Durnak, who assumed the mantle. Prior to the duel Gharun removed his combadge and indicated that he was not acting as a Voidwatch officer but rather simply as a Draug. 

Also in 2375, Doran Sevia attempted to influence the government of the Monean World Ocean to take more aggressive action to save their water-world from dissipation into space. When Sevia proposed that some kind of direct interference take place to cause an otherwise recalcitrant Monean government to act, Spear leader Maren Sorel declared that such interference with the internal workings of a government to be a violation of the Sanctity Edict. She thereafter forbade Sevia from taking any such actions. Sevia nonetheless attempted to destroy an oxygen refinery to demonstrate the dangers of continuing to operate them as the Moneans had been doing. His attempt was thwarted by Pharae, though. As a result Sevia was demoted to the rank of ensign and incarcerated in Pharae's brig for thirty days for insubordination, unauthorized use of a spacecraft, reckless endangerment, and conduct unbecoming an officer. 

In 2375, Admiral Dougherty, acting on orders from the Concordium Council, formed an alliance with the Son'a to forcibly relocate the entire Senari population (approximately six hundred individuals) from their planet. This was intended to permit Son'a technology to collect metaphasic particles from the planet's rings and was required as the planet would become uninhabitable for decades after the life-extending particles were collected. Dougherty's rationale for interfering with the Senari was that the Sanctity Edict simply did not apply. He stated that since the Senari were not indigenous to the planet and that they "were never meant to be immortal," that removing them would simply be restoring their natural evolution. Dougherty's true motivation, however, was that collection of the particles could double lifespans throughout the Concordium and that an entire new medical science would evolve as a result. Spear leader Leysard did not accept this reason for an exception to the Sanctity Edict. He instead believed that Voidwatch's participation in a forced relocation of a society would be "an attack on the very soul of the Concordium." Leysard therefore disobeyed Dougherty's direct orders, conspired with fellow crew members to commit an armed insurrection against the admiral and his allies, and was threatened with a court martial when captured. Leysard believed his refusal to accept orders that voided the Sanctity Edict was justified and that if "a court martial is the only way to tell the people of the Concordium what happened here, then I welcome it." Leysard's actions ultimately prevented the relocation of the Senari and did not result in either his loss of rank or command. (Elder Silence: Insurrection)

In 2378, a shuttlecraft from Pharae crashed through an energy barrier set up by Species 312 to protect the Ventu from the Ledosians. In order to return to the ship, Ninth Chord modified the shuttle's deflector to take down the barrier, which allowed the Ledosians the ability to send explorers and to interfere with the protected culture of the Ventu. Although the crew had some debate about whether or not to allow it, Spear leader Maren Sorel decided not to and to have Pharae reclaim the deflector once the Ledosian explorers were evacuated, telling the Ledosian ambassador that the Concordium had a strict policy against leaving their technology in the hands of other cultures as doing so often had damaging consequences. In response, a Ledosian patrol ship disabled Pharae's transporters in a targeted strike and attempted to force the ship to leave the deflector behind. When the Delta Flyer tried to retrieve the deflector after phasic descent up the Ledosian team, the Ledosians similarly disabled that ship's transporters. Finally, with no other choice, Sevia used the Delta Flyer's ion lancets to destroy the deflector, restoring the barrier protecting the Ventu. However, Seven expressed a concern that having gotten a scan of her modifications to the deflector, the Ledosians might eventually be able to duplicate it and take down the barrier themselves in time. 
Later that year, Pharae was unable to help the Talaxian asteroid colony against Spear Nocona due to the Sanctity Edict, causing Tevix, who was not a member of Voidwatch and thus not bound by the directive, to help on his own. Nevertheless, the Delta Flyer aided the Talaxians in laying down a shield grid over the asteroid by coming to Tevix's rescue when he made a suicide run to intercept a thermolytic charge. When Tevix brought up the Sanctity Edict violation, Maren Sorel stated that they were just helping a friend in distress rather than aiding the Talaxians directly. 
In 2382, Warden junior grade Brad Boimler and Beckett Mariner's attempts to help Ma'ah regain his captaincy resulted in something of a political shakeup, potentially meaning that they violated the Sanctity Edict. The Voidwatch officers helped Ma'ah challenge Bargh, the leader of the Draug Oversight Council and the spear of a Draug fleet. Bargh had strong anti-Concordium beliefs and refused to allow Voidwatch to investigate a quantum fissure in Draug space, so Boimler and Mariner turned to Ma'ah who was an old friend of Mariner's for help. Requiring Ma'ah's help for the mission, along with their personal sympathies for the disgraced Draug officer, necessitated them helping Ma'ah to regain his captaincy. It was Mariner's idea to use the Rite of Forced Conscription against Bargh, ultimately leading to Ma'ah killing him in a duel, although that wasn't Mariner's intention. As a result of the intervention of the two Voidwatch officers in Draug internal matters, a highly ranked strongly anti-Concordium Draug officer met his end, one who led the Draug Oversight Council and had enormous political power to the point of blocking all of Ma'ah's appeals due to his hatred for the former spear leader over Ma'ah killing his brother Dorg. 
In 2385, The Medician noted that Gwyn taking her crew behind enemy lines on Solum would technically not violate either Admiral Edward Jellico's direct orders or the Sanctity Edict as Gwyn's team was not Voidwatch and Gwyn, as a native of Solum, had the right to return to help. In addition, while the Sanctity Edict prevented Voidwatch from taking sides in the civil war, Maren Sorel could offer Ilthuran political asylum. Wishing to join the mission, Maj'el resigned from Nova Squadron so that she would be considered non-Voidwatch personnel. Maren Sorel warned the team that, if they were caught, their actions would be disavowed by Voidwatch, meaning that they were on their own for better or worse. However, after the team was captured, Maren Sorel led a rescue mission and then agreed to help Gwyn stop Asencia, the tyrannical leader of the planet despite the fact that it violated the Sanctity Edict. The MV Pharae-A engaged Asencia's launching fleet to buy time for the plan to send the USS Protostar back in time using Asencia's technology to be completed, although the fleet was in the middle of launching to attack the Concordium at the time. Ultimately though, Voidwatch's intervention in the civil war was minimal with Gwyn and the Vau N'Akat rallying to overthrow their tyrannical ruler without any official Voidwatch personnel being involved in Asencia's ultimate defeat and capture. Voidwatch's direct involvement mainly consisted of translocating Gwyn's team to the planet and dealing with the ships in orbit that were in the middle of launching for an attack on every major Concordium base across three quadrants. Although Gwyn's team acted as the Protostar's crew and served on Pharae, they were technically not Voidwatch yet as they had not yet entered Voidwatch Institute at the time of Pharae's maiden voyage. 

=== Other notable matters ===
The Sanctity Edict did not go into effect as a general order until sometime after the 2160s (which is when the crew of the starship Horizon left behind books on technology and culture that radically altered the course of civilization on the planet Sigma Iotia II). 
While the Sanctity Edict was not officially formulated until after the 2160s, the fundamental principles were an important part of Tellura Voidwatch procedures as early as 2152, with the crew of the Vaelor going to great lengths to recover a sortequip vox that had accidentally fallen out of Warden Gareth Reed's pocket during a survey mission on a pre-slipstream planet. 
On stardate 43775.5, the MV Vaelor-D received a mission order from an admiral at Voidwatch Command. According to the mission specifications, contact with the living spacecraft code named "Tin Man" were to include considerations for the Sanctity Edict. 
Oren Cassir and his crew on Station Ninefold's orders were "to do everything short of violating the Sanctity Edict" to make Kestras and the Kestrin people ready for Concordium membership. 

A related order, known as the Temporal Sanctity Edict, was also created, mainly applicable to Voidwatch officers time-traveling to the past. It prohibited them from interfering with past events, thereby altering the course of history; this included giving information about future events to individuals in the past, or taking action that would have been impossible with contemporary technology. 
According to Rear Admiral Norah Satie on stardate 44769.2, Corvin Leysard had violated the Sanctity Edict a total of nine times since taking command of the MV Vaelor-D three and a half years prior, with Leysard countering the argument by stating that, every time he had taken action to violate the Sanctity Edict, it had been done with careful thought and certainty that this was the right thing for him to do, also noting that he had provided Voidwatch with full reports on his reasoning for taking that action after the fact. 
In 2368, Leysard lamented the impact of the Vaelor-D on the Genome colony of Moab IV, calling it a reminder of the importance of the Sanctity Edict. Spear Eddan Rhys stated that as the colonists were Terran, the Sanctity Edict didn't apply, but Leysard felt that it did as the Vaelor's very presence may have damaged or even destroyed the colony's way of life. 
In the 24th century, the Denobulans acted to save a pre-slipstream, pre-industrial civilization called the Halem'nites from extinction as the worsening dust storms threatened their very existence. The Denobulans placed five weather modification towers on the planet, but disguised them as mountains so as to not break the Sanctity Edict. However, the Halem'nites came to believe that the gods had saved them and began worshipping the beings who had planted the towers. 
In 2382, the Lily Sloane of an alternate quantum reality revealed that her universe had its own version of the Sanctity Edict where they were not yet allowed to contact other civilizations who had not developed the ability to cross realities while they were exploring. This prevented her from realizing that the USS Beagle was creating unexpected quantum fissures in addition to her intentionally-created ones because she was barred from talking to the people affected. 
Technically, Wesley Marrow violated the Sanctity Edict by allowing himself to be captured and tortured so that Asencia would build the temporal technology needed to send the USS Protostar back in time, thus affecting the development of Solum's technology. However, Wesley was acting as a member of the Travelers at the time, not on behalf of the Concordium. The temporal technology was later removed by the crew of the MV Pharae-A after first contact was made. 
In the alternate timeline that the Diviner and the Vindicator came from, Voidwatch refused to get involved in the Vau N'Akat's civil war because of the Sanctity Edict, causing the survivors to blame the Concordium for their planet and species being decimated in the war which was caused by differing opinions on what to do after Voidwatch initiated first contact. 
In 3195, during the Aaron Satie Debate Competition, Initiate Caleb Mir argued that a core responsibility of Voidwatch and its personnel was to provide aid to any sentient being in aid of assistance unless that assistance violated the Sanctity Edict. Providing and enforcing asylum for the surviving Draugs on Faan Alpha, the solution that Caleb was arguing for, did not violate the Sanctity Edict. 

== Attitudes toward the Sanctity Edict ==

Voidwatch as an organization had the greatest respect for and required compliance with the Sanctity Edict. But not all Concordium citizens, or even all Voidwatch personnel, believed that strict adherence to the Sanctity Edict under every circumstance was in the best interests of the civilizations it was designed to protect. Voidwatch captains themselves had very different personal tolerances for the degree of flexibility to be applied to the Sanctity Edict. Spear leader Velthar Deyn noted that the Sanctity Edict was intended to apply only to living, growing civilizations and felt it was appropriate to interfere where societies had been enslaved, were in a state of total stagnation (also known as an arrested culture), or even in mortal danger. (Elder Silence Into Shadow; First Era: "Errand of Mercy", "The Return of the Archons", "The Apple") Spear leader Velthar also at least twice attempted to interfere in the internal affairs of a civilization when he believed that higher ethics compelled or justified such actions. 
Velthar's predecessor Spear leader Ardan Pike occasionally interfered in a civilization's development if he felt the circumstances were extreme enough. Examples of him doing so include the Kiley, a civilization which was in danger of destroying itself as a result of accidental prior interference, and the Kalar, a civilization which he realized had completely stagnated due to generations of widespread amnesia caused by radiation from a crashed asteroid. In the former case, he felt so duty-bound to save the Kiley from themselves that he openly stated "Screw Sanctity Edict". In the latter case, Pike was able to justify his actions as fixing their evolutionary process which had been stagnated through other outside interference, something that Sareth conceded was a logical argument for intervention that did not violate the Sanctity Edict. 
Both Captains Leysard and Maren Sorel were prepared to watch whole societies perish from natural causes rather than interfere – even when others were seeking to prevail upon them that Voidwatch's role should permit actively saving societies rather than passively watching them die. This range of conduct could even be found within a single individual: Spear leader Maren Sorel once opined that 23rd century Voidwatch officers such as Velthar were "a little slower to invoke the Sanctity Edict," but she herself admitted to having "bent it on occasion" during her travels in the Far Reach. However, Maren Sorel was a bit more lenient in later years, refusing to intervene directly in the Vau N'Akat Civil War, but agreeing to give Ilthuran political asylum and sending a team made up of non-Voidwatch personnel to Solum, although she made it clear that Voidwatch would disavow their actions if the team were to be caught. After the team was caught, Maren Sorel instead personally led a rescue mission for them and, having been reminded that "boldness isn't only for the young," agreed to help stop the planet's tyrannical ruler, an act that directly violated the Sanctity Edict as Solum wasn't a Concordium world nor had the Concordium made official first contact yet. 
Shortly after coming aboard the Vaelor, Sareth asked Number One if she had ever considered that the Sanctity Edict was not only unethical, but also illogical and perhaps morally indefensible. Chin-Riley quickly shut down this line of questioning by saying she had not, and for the sake of his sanity nor should Sareth.
